# Chronicle of the port

A dated account of how this port was made, from the first session on 2026-09-19 to the release groundwork on 2026-09-30: what was decided, by whom and why, what was found and by which instrument, and what went wrong first and what caught it. It is raw material for the book about the port, not the book. The technical knowledge lives in the other notes of this directory, which the entries cite.

**Sources**, in brackets after each entry:

- a commit by its hash; git's author dates are the times given;
- a note of this directory with its section heading;
- `SPEC.md` or `CONTROLLER.md` (the controllers' handbook) with a section, as they stand at `43e8f54`;
- *memory*: the controllers' running log `night-2026-09-25.md` in the project memory outside the repository (from 2026-09-25 on), or another file of that directory by name (`wof-remake-project.md`, `controller-asks-user.md`, `fable-budget-opus-workers.md`, `german-keyboard.md`, `self-contained-asks.md`, `visible-browser-windows-ok.md`, `wof-book-plan.md`);
- *transcript*: a session's transcript, by the session's name and the time of a message; these too are outside the repository. The user's own words are quoted from them, briefly, where a decision is theirs.

**Times** are local, Central European Summer Time (UTC+2), as in the git history; the transcripts' UTC stamps are converted. Where the memory gives another time for the same event than a commit or a transcript, the commit and the transcript are taken: the controllers wrote the memory's times from their own sense of the clock, which was off by up to half an hour on some evenings.

**Who.** The user, the disk's owner, who leads the project (Mirko, sy2002). Controller sessions, "Controller Instance 1" to "Controller Instance 11", on Fable 5.1 at xhigh effort: the user talks to one controller, which assigns tasks to workers, reviews and merges their work, and alone asks the user for anything. Worker sessions, named after their task, on Opus at xhigh effort: their commits carry "Claude Opus 5" until 2026-09-22 and "Claude Opus 5.5" from the M5 worker on, while the handbook's table says Opus 5. Two early tasks ran on Fable: the reading of open points 4, 6, 7 and 11, and M2. All sessions share one working directory and communicate by message.

## The mistakes and what caught them

**The stick's up and down bits (2026-09-19 to 2026-09-20).** The groundwork, written from reading, named bit 0 of the input byte "stick down" and bit 1 "stick up", and the M0 shell mapped the up key to bit 1. The headless original contradicted it: asked with `JOY1DAT` `0x0100`, the forward switch alone, the original's decoder `read_joy_bits` returns 1, the menus treat that as the cursor-up key, and a take-off held with bit 1 goes over the bow. The specification, the input note, the shell's keys and the harness's letters were turned round the same morning, and a page test now watches what the core is handed. The handbook draws the lesson: the reading mistake was found by an observation, not by a better reader. [b5f4335, e1614f4, eed2748, a36eae6; `re/notes/headless.md`, "Input"; `CONTROLLER.md`, "Models and effort"]

**The last four map records (2026-09-21).** The worker on points 2, 5 and 3 wrote that the last four records of a map are uninitialised on a real machine, resting on the behaviour of the harness's allocation stub. The controller's review read the callee instead: the game's allocator `0x020874` sets bit 16 of the flags it hands `AllocMem`, which is `MEMF_CLEAR`, so they are zero records on the machine as in the harness. What followed for the port is that its arena hands out zeroed memory, with a test. [ba7ea44; `re/notes/map.md`, "The file"; `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "Where mission memory lives"]

**Unicorn's memory-form shift (2026-09-23).** The oracle had been trusted to execute 68000 code as the chip does. An oracle test of `object_step` over random states showed the original under Unicorn bouncing a bomb on an airfield into a vertical speed of plus 9 where a 68000 gives minus 2: Unicorn 2.1.4 decides arithmetic or logical from bit 3 of the opcode, which in the memory form belongs to the address mode. No script reached the bounce. `tools/m68k_fix.py` hooks the executable's three `asr.w d16(An)`, and the controller's survey found no other affected shift. [7e2bd7d; `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The emulator's memory-form shift (observed)"; `CONTROLLER.md`, "Pitfalls that cost time"]

**The wall-clock timeout (2026-09-27 to 2026-09-28).** M2's acceptance was that two runs give identical dumps, and it was believed to hold. The controller's full suite on a loaded machine failed `japcarrier_m`'s two loops once, the oil timer one apart from tick 692, and the two recordings differed with identical inputs and entropy. The mechanism: a slice of emulation ended by Unicorn's wall-clock timeout stops from a timer thread; landing inside a memory hook it makes the next slice execute the instruction again, and between Unicorn's two stop flags it silences every code hook and the run spins for ever. With 3 ms slices every one of ten recordings differed, one crashed and three hung. Slices end by instruction count since. [d7394ae, 2abd0f8; `re/notes/headless.md`, "Unicorn, as it behaves here"; memory, "The harness-determinism report came at 05:45 on 2026-09-28"]

**The shared core between tests (2026-09-27 to 2026-09-28).** A process holds one copy of the core's statics, and each test was believed to find it as it needed it. At M8 part 2 the music tests left the core in a mission with the shapes mirrored and were made to leave a fresh core behind. That reset, running in the serial order between `test_mission`, which left a mission behind too, and the shape oracles, hid a second case: the first parallel run failed oracle tests that pass serially, and the leak was reproduced serially in a chosen order. Every test that takes the core starts from a fresh one since, at 4.7 ms each. [a6e6061, b155b03; `re/notes/testing.md`, "A fresh core for every test"; memory, "Parallel-suite interim at 10:20 on 2026-09-28"]

**The pokes that outlived their test (2026-09-29).** The fresh core was believed to isolate every test. The controller's two-phase run of the M8 left-overs failed the front end's hand-over with `mission_number` 3 on the xdist worker that had run a map c closed loop before it: the replay's poke survived `wof_init`, which does not touch the test instrumentation, and serially the alphabet hid it. Everything that lives beside the core is now put back before every test, and a replay clears its own however it ends. [b4c4559, b122c13; `re/notes/testing.md`, "A fresh core for every test"]

**The process-wide cache of sample starts (2026-09-29).** The comparison names a pointer into a freed block by the nearest sample start below it that the run has seen, and that cache was believed to be the run's. The controller's closed loop of `save_a` on seed 7 at one VBlank a pass found Paula's channel 0 named by another recording's start, `0x0800093D` against `0x0600003D`, depending on which recordings had run before in the process. Every replay empties the cache since, held by a test. [3d3e062; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "Paula's channel 0 after the save"]

**The register carried as a long (2026-09-29).** The port treated the targets' walks as word-sized throughout. The same follow-up found on `save_a` that `target_refill` leaves D1 the direction as a long, and the next target's fire hands its upper word on to the smoke as the x's fraction; no script had exposed it. The port carries the word, `SPEC.md` 7.1 gained a rule, and the oracle tests of M5 to M7 draw D1's upper word at random. [05e8e23; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "The port"; `CONTROLLER.md`, "The plan ahead"]

**The software canvas in Firefox (2026-09-19, and 2026-09-30).** The first shell drew with an accelerated 2D canvas; in GPU-composited Firefox it never showed what `putImageData` wrote, and the user saw black. The canvas that receives the pixels asks to be software-backed since. The two canvases after it were believed accelerated, as `SPEC.md` 6.2 said. M9's measurement in a visible Firefox showed that the smooth reduction onto the displayed canvas runs in software Skia on the main thread: 28.7 ms at the owner's fullscreen size, half the frame rate. The picture is drawn by WebGL since, with the 2D path as the fallback. [transcript, Controller Instance 1, 22:27; fe45c1e; `re/notes/page-video.md`, "Before: the two-step 2D path"]

**The replay that stopped before the last pass's ticks (2026-09-30).** Every mission comparison since M4 was believed to cover its script to the end. M7 part 2's control for a playback that ends one entry late showed the gap: the late end falls in the ticks after the last pass, and `tests/m4compare.py` stopped every script at the original's last pass. The last ticks of every script of M4 to M7 part 1 had never been compared. The replay runs through them since. [92e69e5; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "Part 2: the controls"; `CONTROLLER.md`, "Pitfalls that cost time"]

**The cruise ship's guns for a loaded game (2026-09-30).** M6 had left a loaded game's gun list alone, believing the file carries it whole. M7 part 2's completeness list over `load_hold` found `load_ship_shapes` (`0x01331A`) setting 5 at `+6` for guns 1 and 2 after `ship_guns_setup` for a loaded game too. The port now does the same. [92e69e5; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "Part 2: the port"]

**The 8,192-byte file limit (2026-09-29).** The port's file system took no file larger than 8,192 bytes. M7 part 1's layout of the saved game showed saves of up to 11,516 bytes, on map m; on maps h, i and k to o a save would not have been kept. The limit is 12,412 bytes, the largest save the port's tables allow. [448069f; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "The port"]

**The play screen after a dialog (2026-09-29).** `screen_game_restore` had been ported from reading in M4 and never run with passes after it. `save_a`'s closed loop showed 997 passes differing, in the rows below the sky's split and in the ticker's ramp: the dialog's screen had switched the port's play-screen model off and nothing switched it on again. It is switched on again since. [448069f; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "The port"]

**The fullscreen pause and the lost audio resume (2026-09-26 to 2026-09-27).** The shell was believed to stop and resume cleanly around a hidden page. The user's Firefox, put into fullscreen, stalled and went silent for good. `tests/pagefullscreen.mjs` measured what a window's change of state does: the page hidden and shown again within a few milliseconds got a pause on the way out, and a resume made only once the context read suspended was skipped when the suspend landed after the page was back (9 of 12 tries in Firefox). The M6 build had the same. The pause is asked for on the way back after an absence of a second or more, the suspend and resume go by the shell's own record, and leaving fullscreen pauses, as `SPEC.md` 6.2 had always said. [bf95c78; `re/notes/porting-m8.md`, "The page and the fullscreen finding"]

**The diagnostics key's two codes (2026-09-20, and 2026-09-30).** First the hint named the key by the character on a US keyboard, a backtick, and the user found a caret there; it names the key by its place since. Then the shell took `event.code` `Backquote` for the key left of 1, a positional code believed to be the same in every browser. The user found the overlay would not open in Chrome and Safari: on a Mac with an ISO keyboard Blink and WebKit report that key as `IntlBackslash` and the key right of the left Shift as `Backquote`, while Firefox undoes the swap. Both codes toggle the overlay since. [b9c3d23, 60d3b7f; transcript, Controller Instance 11, 11:32 and 11:44; `CONTROLLER.md`, "Pitfalls that cost time"]

**The builder's home directory in the page (2026-09-30).** The page was believed to hold nothing of the machine that built it. Preparing the release showed that `zig cc` keeps DWARF in `core.wasm`, whose string table held the absolute path of every source and of the build directory: the page named the builder's home and differed with the checkout's place. The build maps the paths to `./src` since. [1d7392e]

## The instruments

- **The oracle** (2026-09-19, `tools/oracle.py`; used from M1 on through `tests/original.py`). It runs a routine of the original executable in Unicorn on chosen inputs, so that the port's routine can be compared with it result, registers, flags and memory: the differential test of every pure routine. [c191167, 60c9491; `SPEC.md` 7.4]
- **The headless original** (2026-09-20, `tools/headless.py`). The whole game from `main` under the oracle, the OS stubbed, the beam position served from the port's entropy stream, VBlanks only where the program waits; it answers what the original does over a scripted input, tick by tick and pass by pass, with dumps, write reports and observers. Grown by the keyboard's ROM path, observers of returns and memory (2026-09-20), Paula (2026-09-26), the music's timer (2026-09-27) and slices by instruction count (2026-09-28). [96c8008; `re/notes/headless.md`; `SPEC.md` 8]
- **The reach map** (2026-09-21, `tools/reach_observe.py`). Which routines and basic blocks a set of scripts executes in the original; everything else becomes a marked stand-in, and `--cold` fails on an unmarked region. It answers what a milestone must port, and makes a script that leaves the ported ground fail loudly. [1b43702; `SPEC.md` 7.4; `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "What decides what is ported: the reach map"]
- **The autopilots** (M4 2026-09-21, M5 2026-09-22, M6 2026-09-26, M7 2026-09-29). Searches over the headless original for inputs that make something happen: a landing, an attack, a ship sunk, a save; their compressed choices are the mission scripts. [306f580, aceac9f, 3a2519b, 448069f]
- **The open and the closed loop** (2026-09-21 and 2026-09-22, `tests/m4compare.py`). In the open loop the port is handed the original's state before every pass, so a difference names its pass; in the closed loop the port runs alone from the program's start and only the seed is shared, which is the definition of faithful. [7715e16, 306f580; `SPEC.md` 8, row "Mission, pass by pass"]
- **The completeness list** (2026-09-21, `tests/m4complete.py`). Every address the original writes during a mission must be registered state, compared another way or listed with its reason and milestone; it found the cruise ship's guns. [0d3b914; `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The completeness list"]
- **The controls** (2026-09-21 on). Before a comparison is believed, the port is broken on purpose, in a named place, and the comparison must fail at the named step; the pass-rate control of points 2, 5 and 3, then each milestone's set, "six controls caught" in M7. A control that did not fail found the replay's missing last ticks. [ba7ea44; `re/notes/passes.md`, "The control: one, two and three VBlanks per pass"; `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "How the port is held to the original"; `re/notes/porting-m7.md`, "Part 2: the controls"]
- **The Paula model** (2026-09-26, `tools/headless_paula.py` and `src/audio.c`, one definition). The audio registers, DMA, the channel-end interrupt and the sound event log, so that the effects and later the music could be compared event by event. [720280a; `re/notes/sound.md`]
- **The film of the real machine** (2026-09-22, `tools/film_rate.py`). The user's 240-frame film of their PAL Amiga, with the story scroller as the calibration; it answered how many VBlanks a pass takes, 2 in a quiet scene. [d15b12a; `re/notes/passes.md`, "What the film of the real machine shows"]
- **The page tests with a true scale factor** (2026-09-20, `tests/picture.py`, `tests/pagescale.mjs`). Every framebuffer pixel against a screenshot, the picture's position fitted from its edges, the hardness of the edges, under `--force-device-scale-factor=2`, because an emulated density shifts the canvas; since M9 on both renderers, with a frame-time test at the screen's size (`tests/pageframes.mjs`). [73b8697, 5a9c1c4; `SPEC.md` 8, row "Page"]
- **The parallel suite** (2026-09-28). The emulator tests over the cores and the page tests alone after them, held to the serial reference's outcome set by `tools/junit_compare.py`; it brought the suite from three hours to about one, and exposed two isolation leaks. [7013e61, af4b1f5, b155b03, 2a69eda; `re/notes/testing.md`]
