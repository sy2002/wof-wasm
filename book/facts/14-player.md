# Fact sheet: chapter 14, The player

Every claim the chapter makes, one line each, with its source, in the order of the chapter. A claim without a source goes to the list at the end and stays out of the draft. A general fact about the machine or the world that the notes do not state is sourced in a reference work and marked **(reference)**; what the game does always comes from the notes, the listing and the port's sources. Each mechanism ends with what the port made of it and, in one plain sentence, the instrument of Part I that holds it; no "How we know" box (`book/BOOK.md` 4, point 6; the controller's task).

Counts are of the branch's base, `fcc7963`, counted in the files named, by reading or with the command in the counts table at the end. Run for a fact, each on one core: the headless original over the M4 scripts to count their ticks (`tools/m4_scripts.py`'s `script()` through `headless.Headless`, 5 to 16 seconds a script, the fuel flight 43); one run of the landing script in the headless original with the player read after every tick (8 seconds), which also kept the run's schedule; the native library replaying that schedule (under a second); `extract_tables.Image` for the frame tables and the small tables of the player's code; `ppkc.parse` for the frames' sizes. No test run, no browser, no rebuild of the native library.

Words used in the sheet as the chapter will use them: **left** is west, towards smaller world x, and the facing −1; **right** is east and +1. **Height** is the record's y, pixels above the water line (chapter 13). The **pitch** is kept in hundredths of a degree, so 600 is 6 degrees. "Forward" is the stick pushed away, the input byte's bit 0, the letter U of the scripts, as chapters 7 and 8 name it; the manual's diagram puts it at the top.

## 1. What the game keeps of the player

3. The player's aircraft is one record of `0x1E` bytes, thirty, at `0x025078`; `player_record` (`0x027DEC`) always points at it. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The player's record"; `src/player.c`, the opening comment; `src/mission.def` (`WOF_TABLE(player, player, 1, 0x025078)`); the record's size counted in `src/records.def` through `m4state.Layout(...).records['player']['orig_size']` = 30 (counts table).
4. Its fields: the height (`+0x00`, pixels above the water); the x (`+0x02`, world pixels); the frame (`+0x04` a pointer to the frame's shape record, `+0x08` its name); the state (`+0x0C`); the fuel (`+0x0E`); a word drawn at random at the reset and constant afterwards (`+0x10`); the oil (`+0x12`); the facing (`+0x14`, +1 or −1, the horizontal speed multiplied by it); the horizontal speed (`+0x16`) and the vertical speed (`+0x18`), pixels a tick; a word the crash clears (`+0x1A`); the enemy's countdown (`+0x1C`, ticks). Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The player's record", the table; `src/records.def` through `m4state` (the field `w1a` at 26, counts table); `src/player.c`, `wof_crash` (`P.w1a = 0`). The notes name no use of `+0x10` or `+0x1A`; the chapter says two words are left aside, without a purpose.
5. The state word selects what the tick does with the aircraft: 0 in the air; 1 on the deck or in the hold; 4 coming down; 6 in the sea; 7 held by a cable; 8 burning, a wreck at rest; 11 the lift moving; 2, 3, 5, 9 and 10 never set. Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The player's states", the table; `src/player.c`, the comment of `0x01C660` and `wof_player_update`'s chain of cases.
6. The two position rules: in the air the x moves by the horizontal speed times the facing and the height by the vertical speed, every tick, with the speed the tick ends with; on the deck and the lift the deck's code moves it instead, and at the ceiling the height is clamped. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The player's record" (the two position rules and their exceptions).
7. Beside the record, globals: `airspeed` (`0x025414`), which scales both speeds; `airspeed_step` (`0x027DEA`), how much the airspeed moves a tick; `pitch_angle` (`0x025AA2`), in hundredths of a degree, and `pitch_target` (`0x025402`), what it eases towards; `pitch_step` (`0x025F16`), how far the stick moves the target a tick, 600 through every flight, written only by the cheat keys `i` and `k` (50 up or down), a tuning value; `attitude_index` (`0x02540E`), 0 to 25, the stage of a turn; `landing_stall` (`0x025AAA`). Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, the globals' table; `re/notes/ffp.md`, "`player_motion`", the inputs; `src/globals.def`.
8. The airspeed is not a speed in any unit of the world: no control sets it directly, it rises by its step to a ceiling of 1,400 and falls to 1,000, and below 1,000 the aircraft sinks. The manual's controls are the stick and the button; the horizontal positions of its stick are named as throttle settings (page 5). `object_spawn` copies it into a new object's `+0x28`, so a shot takes the aircraft's speed with it. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, the globals' table (`airspeed`); `original/manual.txt` page 5 (the stick's diagram: full throttle left and right, three-quarter throttle in the middle). See "Where a source was wrong or silent": the task's "which no control sets".

## 2. The flight model, once per tick

9. `player_motion` (`0x01BDFA`) runs once per tick from the player's update (`0x01C70E`), in the air. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, "`player_motion` `0x01BDFA`, once per tick from `0x01C70E`"; `src/player.c`, `wof_player_update` (state 0 calls `flight_controls`, `wof_player_motion`, `ground`).
10. The pitch eases a quarter of the way towards its target each tick, `divs.w` truncated towards zero; so it stops a few hundredths short (the landing's trace: 2,997 for a target of 3,000). Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic (`pitch_angle += (pitch_target - pitch_angle) / 4`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BE24`–`0x01BE32`; the landing's trace (counts table).
11. While `landing_stall` is set and the target stands at 600, the pitch eases towards −800 (`0xFCE0`) instead. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the inputs' table; `re/notes/objects.md` (`landing_stall`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BE02`–`0x01BE22`; `src/player.c`, `wof_player_motion`.
12. The angle is the pitch plus a small offset of the tick (`pitch_delta`), taken without its sign; its sine and cosine come from a table of 91 sines in whole degrees, `sine_degrees` (`0x025B74`), the cosine as the sine of 90 less the angle. Entries 1 to 3 repeat the sine of 4 degrees. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic and "The two tables of constants"; `re/tables.toml` (`sine_degrees`, 91 longs).
13. The across component takes the sign of the pitch. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md` (`if pitch_angle < 0 or pitch_delta < 0: across = SPNeg(across)`).
14. The horizontal speed is the airspeed times the cosine times the attitude's factor, plus 50, over 100, cut to a whole number (`SPFlt`, two `SPMul`, `SPAdd`, `SPDiv`, `SPFix`); the x moves by it times the facing (`muls.w`, the low word kept). Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BEC4`–`0x01BF28`; `src/player.c`. At a level 1,400, 14 pixels a tick: the landing's trace (the deck roll at 1,400 gives `(1400 + 50) / 100` = 14; in the air hc05 level at 1,400 the trace's x steps 14 a tick, ticks 352 to 360).
15. `attitude_factor` (`0x025B0C`) is 26 factors, one an attitude: 1 at 0, falling ever faster to 0.2 at 13, 0 at 14, climbing back to 0.98 at 25; so mid-turn the aircraft has no horizontal speed. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, "The two tables of constants"; decoded from the executable through `tests/ffp_model.py`'s `ATTITUDE` (counts table).
16. The vertical speed is the airspeed times the sine, over 100, cut to a whole number. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BF2C`–`0x01BF50`.
17. Before the speeds, the routine takes the across component, cut to a whole number by `SPFix`, out of `airspeed_step`. The sine is below 1 at every angle short of 90 degrees and `SPFix` truncates towards zero, so this takes 0 at every pitch the game reaches: the pitch's target is held between −4,500 and 3,000 by the controls (claims 33, 35) and turned round at the ceiling, at most 4,500 then, and the angles **observed** in the 298 kept entries run from −4,497 to 2,997. So climbing costs no airspeed through this line. The chapter says it as a fact of the arithmetic, the table's values and the observed angles, in one sentence (the controller's note 1). Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic and "What each operation does at its edges" (`SPFix` of anything between −1 and 1 is 0); `src/player.c`, `flight_controls` (the clamps `0xEE6C` and `0xBB8`); the 298 entries of `tests/ffp_observed.json` (counts table). See "Where a source was wrong or silent": the task's "so that climbing costs speed and diving gives it".
18. Flying right below an airspeed of 1,000, the step also loses a tenth of itself each tick. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md` (`if record[+0x14] > 0 and airspeed < 1000: airspeed_step -= airspeed_step / 10`). Sheet only, or a clause.
19. Below an airspeed of 1,000, in the air, the vertical speed loses (1,000 less the airspeed) over 100, and, unless the stick is held forward (bit 0 of the tick's input byte), the pitch's target loses half a step a tick, down to −4,500. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic (`0x026D43 bit 0` is the low byte of `tick_input`, `src/player.c`, the `INPUT` macro; bit 0 the stick forward, `re/notes/input.md`, "Input byte layout"); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BF54`–`0x01BF9C`.
20. The height then moves by the vertical speed; above 1,100 (`0x44C`) it is set to 1,100, the pitch's target is turned round and the vertical speed halved and turned round, so the aircraft bounces off a ceiling; below −4 it is held at −4. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic; `src/player.c`, `wof_player_motion`; `re/notes/objects.md` (at the ceiling exactly four ticks of the climb script).
21. The motion uses six of the nine operations of the ROM's fast floating point (`SPFlt`, `SPMul`, `SPAdd`, `SPDiv`, `SPFix`, `SPNeg`), twelve calls a tick; the game's only other use in the tick is an enemy aircraft's motion. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.4, the mathffp row; `re/notes/ffp.md`, "The call sites" (twelve sites `0x01BE90` to `0x01BF48`, the neg one only when the pitch is negative).
22. The constants `0xC8000046` and `0xC8000047` are 50 and 100 in that format. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, the arithmetic's last line. Sheet; the listing shows them.
23. Why the port keeps the arithmetic bit for bit: a browser's floating point rounds differently and the state would drift; the positions and speeds are compared after every tick in both loops (chapter 8), so a last bit that differed would show at once and carry on in the closed loop. Source: `SPEC.md` 7.1 ("Never substitute float or double; rounding differs and the state would drift"); `book/docs/part-1/oracle.md`, "The ROM's own floating point"; `book/docs/part-1/mission.md` ("every variable and table the port keeps", after every tick).
24. The port: `wof_player_motion` calls `wof_ffp_mul` and its siblings of `src/ffp.h`, the ROM's routines transliterated into integer code (chapter 5). Source: `src/player.c`, `wof_player_motion`; `src/ffp.h`; `re/notes/ffp.md`, "The port".
25. The model: `tests/ffp_model.py`'s `model_01bdfa` is the motion in Python, taking a floating-point engine as an argument, so the ROM or the port can drive it; it reproduces every one of the 14,861 entries observed in ten flights, of which 298 are kept in `tests/ffp_observed.json`. Source: `re/notes/ffp.md`, "`player_motion`" (the observed entries); `tests/ffp_model.py`, the module docstring.
26. The instrument: `player_motion` over 3,000 random states (every attitude, both facings, the ceiling, the airspeed's floor, the bounds of the step) against the original under the oracle, the original's floating point served by the ROM's own routines, and against the model driven by the port's floating point. Source: `tests/test_oracle_m4.py`, `test_player_motion_matches_the_original_and_the_model` (its docstring, `range(3000)`, the `FFP_LVO` hooks calling `ffp.Reference`, `d.ported.ffp` as the model's engine).
27. A control: the motion's +50 before the division made +25 in the port; the oracle test's case 7 leaves the x and the horizontal speed one lower. Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "How the port is held to the original", the controls. Optional, a clause.
28. Figure `flight-model.svg` (new, hand-drawn): the pitch angle, the airspeed split into a speed along (the cosine, times the attitude's factor) and a speed across (the sine), both over 100; a height axis from the floor of −4 through the sea at 0 to the ceiling of 1,100, the bounce; an airspeed bar from 1,000 to 1,400 with the region below 1,000, where the aircraft sinks. Its numbers: 1,000, 1,400, 1,100, −4, 100, all claims 8, 14, 16, 19, 20.

## 3. The stick

29. The stick reaches the logic through the input byte (chapter 7): four bits for forward, back, right and left, and two for the button, held and tapped. Source: `re/notes/input.md`, "Input byte layout"; `book/docs/part-1/time.md`.
30. In the air (`0x01BFF4`), the stick left alone: a turn in progress goes on (past attitude 6) or unwinds (at 6 or below), the nose comes down towards level (the target falls by a step to 0, or while turning by a quarter step, down to −2,250), the step falls by one to a floor of 4 and the airspeed falls by its step, not below 1,000. Source: `src/player.c`, the comment and code of `0x01BFF4`, `flight_controls`; `re/notes/objects.md` (`airspeed_step` floor 4).
31. Towards the facing: the step rises by one to 8 and the airspeed by the step, to 1,400; a turn past attitude 6 is finished, one at 6 or below unwinds. The manual calls the stick's horizontal positions full throttle left and right and the middle three-quarter throttle (page 5). Source: `src/player.c`, `flight_controls` (`0x01C0F6`); `original/manual.txt` page 5.
32. Against the facing: a turn (claim 37).
33. With a direction, forward raises the pitch's target by a step (above an airspeed of 1,000; a quarter step flying right and an eighth flying left below it), to 3,000; back lowers it by a step (half while turning), to −4,500; neither, while turning, lowers it by a quarter step to −2,250, so a turn loses height, as the manual warns (page 5). Source: `src/player.c`, `flight_controls` (`0x01C176`, `0x01C21E`); `original/manual.txt` page 5 (turning loses altitude).
34. Back alone lowers the target by two steps, a steep dive. Source: `src/player.c`, `flight_controls` (`0x01C288`).
35. Forward alone while flying left eases the target to 600 and sets `landing_stall`; every other input clears it. Flying right, forward alone lowers the target instead, towards −600. Source: `re/notes/objects.md` (`landing_stall`); `src/player.c`, `flight_controls`; `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The scripts of part 2" (the turns script sets it).
36. The stall: with the flag set and the target at 600, the motion eases the pitch towards −800 (claim 11), so the aircraft sinks while the frame, which follows the target (claim 41), shows the nose raised. The manual's diagram names the stick's top position the stall and its landing stalls the aircraft so that it drops onto the deck (pages 5 and 6). Source: claims 11, 41; `original/manual.txt` pages 5 and 6; the landing's trace (ticks 356 to 359: the target 600, the pitch −705 to −746, the height falling 39, 38, 37).
37. A turn (`0x01AB80`): the attitude counts from 0 to 25, a step every second call, one call a tick; at 14 the facing changes; past 25 it is 0 again and the turn is done. Without the argument `whole` (the stick left alone, or still against the facing), past 19 it folds to 26 less itself, which has the same frame, and unwinds from there. Source: `src/player.c`, the comment and code of `0x01AB80`, `turn_step`; the frame tables (claim 39: entries 20 to 25 repeat entries 6 to 1).
38. A turn is refused in the air while an enemy aircraft of a certain state sits at attitude 13 (`0x01AA6E`); chapter 16's. Source: `src/player.c`, `turn_allowed`. Sheet only.
39. During a turn the frame comes from one of two tables of 26 names, one for each facing (`frames_left` `0x025D10`, `frames_right` `0x025D78`), by the attitude, drawn as stored: a turn from facing left shows `hc28` to `hc32`, then, facing right from attitude 14, `hc32`, `hc33` to `hc36`, and `hc27` back to `hc22`. Source: `src/player.c`, `wof_aircraft_frame` (case 0 with an attitude: the name returned without `mark_both`); `re/tables.toml` (`frames_left`, `frames_right`); the names read from the executable (counts table).
40. In level flight the frame comes from `frames_flight` (`0x025DE0`) by the pitch's target, `(target + 5000) / 500`, and is mirrored in place to the facing through the marker (chapter 12); on the deck from `frames_deck` (`0x025CE0`) by the attitude, mirrored the same way. Source: `src/player.c`, `frame_select` (`0x01C378`) and `wof_aircraft_frame` (`mark_both`); `re/tables.toml`; `book/docs/part-2/shapes.md`, "The mirror".
41. So the frame of level flight shows where the pitch is going, its target, not where it stands. Source: claim 40 (`up` from `pitch_target`). *(reasoning from the code, marked)*
42. Figure `turn-hellcat.png` (`shapes` maker, `pick`): the 21 frames of a turn from facing left to facing right, in the order the attitude shows them, `hc28` … `hc36`, `hc27` … `hc22`, from `hellcat.shp`. Source: claim 39. Why these: the turn's own frames are drawn as stored, so the sheet shows exactly what the screen shows; the level frames are chapter 12's (`mirror-hc05.png`).
43. Above a height of 186 the player's update selects the eighth-scale view for the next pass (chapter 11). Source: `src/player.c`, `wof_player_update` (`P.y > 0xBA`); `book/docs/part-2/display.md`. A clause.
44. The aircraft is drawn at screen x 160 and at the horizon's row less its height (chapter 13). Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines" (`0x0103A6`); `src/world.c`, `wof_draw_player`; `book/docs/part-2/world.md`. A clause, linked.

## 4. The deck and the take-off

45. On the deck (`0x01C4E8`): the stick towards the facing raises the step by one to 8 and the airspeed by the step, to 1,400 (a turn on the spot unwinds first); against the facing the step is 0 and the airspeed falls by 8, and, stopped, the aircraft turns round on the spot, its attitude up to 6 and then the facing flipped; left alone the airspeed falls by 8 a tick, not below 0. Source: `src/player.c`, the comment and code of `0x01C4E8`.
46. The roll (`0x01BDBA`): on the deck the airspeed moves the aircraft, the horizontal speed (airspeed + 50) over 100. Source: `src/player.c`, `deck_roll`; `re/notes/objects.md` (13 ticks of the roll where x advances by one more than the speed is that rounding).
47. The deck's two ends (`deck_west` `0x0253FC`, `deck_east` `0x0253FE`) are the carrier's span in world x, sixteen pixels in from both ends; in map a 6,608 and 7,344. Off either end (`0x01C5F4`) the aircraft is in the air, state 0; on the deck its wheels stand on the deck's height. Source: `src/mission.c`, `wof_deck_span` (`0x01B7BC`); `src/player.c`, `deck_edge`; the landing's trace (counts table).
48. The take-off: the airspeed the roll reached carries into the air; below 1,000 the aircraft sinks (claim 19) until the stick towards the facing has brought it above 1,000; in the landing script it left the deck at 708, sank from a height of 37 to 18 and then climbed. Too slow, or pointed down, it comes down into the sea (porting-m4.md's first way of losing an aircraft). Source: the landing's trace (counts table: ticks 156 to 180); `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "Every way an aircraft is lost" (leaving an end of the deck too slowly to climb).
49. The manual tells the player to push the stick fully towards the way the aircraft faces and, near the deck's end, up as well (page 5). Source: `original/manual.txt` page 5. Cited, not quoted.

## 5. The landing

50. The approach comes from the right with the aircraft facing left, as the manual teaches (page 6). Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The scripts of part 2"; `original/manual.txt` page 6; `book/docs/part-1/mission.md` (told; linked).
51. The ground routine (`0x01BA80`) acts only in the air and only when the aircraft touches, its wheels at or below the ground under it, and their distance below the reference point (the frame's) under 55 (`0x37`). Source: `src/player.c`, `ground` and `touches` (`0x01B8C4`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BAF0`–`0x01BB14`.
52. Over the deck: the x within the deck's two ends, the attitude 0 (level, not turning), the aircraft's wheels no more than 4 pixels below the deck's height, and the carrier afloat (`+0x04` of its record). Then it lands, state 1, only facing left with `landing_stall` set; anything else over the deck bounces: the vertical speed and the pitch's target turned round and the aircraft 6 pixels up. Both sound the screech. Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "Landing, refuelling, rearming", the touch-down; `src/player.c`, the comment and code of `0x01BA80`; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01BAA0`–`0x01BB7E` (`g_0254dc` the carrier's `+0x04`).
53. Away from the deck, touching is a crash: state 4, the airspeed set to the horizontal speed times 100, on land or a ship's deck an explosion and the record under it hit, then the crash's routine (section 8). Source: `src/player.c`, `ground` (`0x01BBC4`); `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, the touch-down.
54. Listing pair: `ground_contact` from `0x01BB18` to `0x01BB7E` (the landing or the bounce) beside `ground` from `if (deck) {` to its `return;`. Source: `re/Wings.lst`; `src/player.c`.
55. The cable (`0x01B92E`), on the deck: at an airspeed of 600 (`0x258`) or more and with the flag `0x025A9C` clear, the hook, 24 pixels (`0x18`) behind the aircraft, within 8 of one of four cables, 56 (`0x38`) apart from 70 (`0x46`) east of the start's x, catches: state 7; the cable's x is kept for the drawing (`0x026D3A`). Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "Landing, refuelling, rearming", the cable; `src/player.c`, `hook`; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01B92E`–`0x01B9BA`.
56. The start's x (`player_start_x`) is where the aircraft stands on the lift; in map a 7,032, so the cables lie at 7,102, 7,158, 7,214 and 7,270. Source: `book/docs/part-2/world.md` (the player's start, map a 7,032); claim 55's arithmetic; the landing's trace (the cable caught at 7,270, `0x026D3A`).
57. The flag `0x025A9C`: on the deck, set whenever the airspeed is above 600 and the stick is not forward, cleared otherwise; while set the aircraft is drawn in the frame of level flight (`frames_flight` entry 9, `hc05`) instead of its deck frame, and the hook does not catch. Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The scripts of part 2" (the landing: "on the deck `0x01C4E8` sets `0x025A9C` whenever the airspeed is above 600 and the stick is not forward, and the hook catches only while `0x025A9C` is clear"); `src/player.c`, `deck_controls` and `wof_aircraft_frame` (case 1: `f = 9` from `0x025DE0`); the name read from the executable (counts table).
58. So the stick must stay forward after the touch-down: the autopilot's finding (chapter 8). Source: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The scripts of part 2"; `tools/m4_autopilot.py`'s docstring; `book/docs/part-1/mission.md` (told there; the chapter gives the mechanism).
59. Held by the cable (state 7), the airspeed falls by 110 (`0x6E`) a tick, the aircraft rolled by it, the pitch's target 0; below 0 it is 0 and the state 1 again. Source: `src/player.c`, `wof_player_update` (`0x01C882`); `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The player's states" (state 7). See "Where a source was wrong or silent": the task's "falls fast and then slowly".

Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines" (`0x0103A6`, "the only lines"); `book/docs/part-1/mission.md` ("the routine that draws a line runs in the landing alone, while the aircraft hangs on the arresting cable").

## Terms and glossary entries

New, in bold with an entry, "First met and defined in chapter 14." (no earlier chapter links or bolds them; chapters 7, 8 and 13 say "the lift", "the hold", "the arresting cable" and "the airspeed" in plain text, which the build does not count):

- Player's record: the thirty bytes the game keeps of the player's aircraft: its height and x, its frame, its state word, fuel, oil, facing, two speeds and the enemy's countdown. The detail: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The player's record".
- Airspeed: the number, 0 to 1,400, that scales the aircraft's two speeds; the stick towards the facing raises it, and in the air below 1,000 the aircraft sinks. The detail: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The player's record". Elsewhere: Wikipedia, "Airspeed".
- Pitch: the aircraft's angle, in hundredths of a degree, which eases each tick a quarter of the way to a target the stick moves. The detail: `re/notes/ffp.md`, "`player_motion`". Elsewhere: Wikipedia, "Aircraft principal axes".
- Attitude: the stage of a turn, 0 to 25, which picks the turn's frame and a factor for the horizontal speed; the facing changes at 14. The detail: `src/player.c`, `0x01AB80`; `re/notes/ffp.md`, "The two tables of constants".
- Landing stall: the flag the stick forward alone sets while the aircraft flies left, which lowers the pitch while the frame shows the nose raised, and without which the aircraft cannot land on the deck. The detail: `re/notes/objects.md`, the globals.
- Arresting cable: one of four cables across the carrier's deck that stop the aircraft when its hook catches one. The detail: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "Landing, refuelling, rearming". Elsewhere: Wikipedia, "Arresting gear".
- Lift: the carrier's lift between the deck and the hold, where the aircraft is refuelled, repaired and rearmed. The detail: `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "Landing, refuelling, rearming".

## Where a source was wrong or silent

- The task: "the airspeed, which scales everything and which no control sets". The stick towards the facing raises it by its step to 1,400, in the air and on the deck (`src/player.c`, `flight_controls` `0x01C0F6`, `deck_controls`), and the manual names the stick's horizontal positions as throttle settings (page 5). `re/notes/objects.md`'s "The game has no throttle control" means no control apart from the stick. The chapter says no control sets it directly and the stick towards the facing raises it.
- The task: "the across component taken out of the airspeed's step, so that climbing costs speed and diving gives it". The line takes `SPFix` of the sine, which is 0 at every pitch the game reaches (claim 17); climbing costs no airspeed through it. The chapter says what the line does and that it changes nothing at the game's angles.
- The task: "below an airspeed of 1,000 the aircraft sinks and the nose drops, the stall of the manual's page 6". Two mechanisms: the sinking below 1,000 (claim 19), which in practice follows a slow take-off, since in the air the controls never take the airspeed below 1,000 (claims 30, 31); and the manual's stall, the stick forward alone while flying left (claims 35, 36), which the landing needs. The chapter tells them apart.
- The task: "the cable's state, in which the airspeed falls fast and then slowly". The airspeed falls by 110 a tick until it is 0, then state 1 (claim 59); the note's "falls by `0x6E` a tick, then 1" names the state that follows.
- The task: "the button holds it at 750 at most". A tick with a fire bit raises the countdown to 750 when it has fallen below, and leaves it above 750 alone (`src/player.c`, `enemy_countdown`); the comment of `0x01BC02` in `src/player.c` and the listing's header say "holds it at 750". The chapter says "puts it back to 750".
- The task and `re/notes/objects.md`: 1,349 ticks. The reset sets 1,350 (`0x546`, `src/mission.c`, `player_reset`); 1,349 is what the observer saw at a mission's start, after the setup's tick. The chapter says 1,350 for each new aircraft.

- `tests/ffp_model.py`, the docstring and comments of `model_01bdfa`: they call `0x025AA2` "the wind", `0x025408` "the drift" and `0x027DEA` "the fuel"; they are `pitch_angle`, `pitch_delta` and `airspeed_step` (`src/globals.def`; `re/notes/ffp.md`).
