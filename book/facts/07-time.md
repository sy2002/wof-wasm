## Opening

1. By the chapter's end the reader knows the game's three clocks (the VBlank, the logic tick, the pass) and what each is for; how the stick reaches the logic through one byte every fourth VBlank; why a pass is not only a picture and what crosses from a pass into a tick; why the schedule is an input of the simulation and what the comparison at three pass rates showed; how a film of a real Amiga at 240 frames a second measured two VBlanks a pass; how the port keeps time; and why the fade step is the one setting of time no measurement stands behind. Source: `book/BOOK.md` 3, chapter 7; the controller's task; claims below.

## Three clocks

2. The VBlank is the machine's beat: the moment the beam finishes a picture, 50 times a second on PAL and 60 on NTSC; it reaches the program as an interrupt. Source: chapter 2, "The VBlank"; `book/docs/glossary.md`, VBlank; **(reference: HRM, chapter 7, the vertical blank interrupt)**. Linked, not redefined.
3. The game counts VBlanks in its own VBlank server, `vblank_server` (`0x011754`), a routine the operating system calls at every VBlank; the sound effects engine's `soundfx_vblank` (`0x01EC64`) is the other one, called before it. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, "Timing is a fixed-step simulation driven by an input queue" ("The VBlank interrupt server `vblank_server` (`0x011754`) counts VBlanks"); `re/notes/headless.md`, "Scheduling" (the two servers, priorities 30 and −10, highest first); `re/Wings.lst` `0x011754`, the header (`asm. "Interrupt_Server": every 4th VBlank sample one input byte into the queue`); chapter 2 ("the game has the operating system call two routines of its own at every one"); **(reference: ROM Kernel Reference Manual: Exec, "Interrupt Servers": one of possibly many routines invoked for one interrupt, called in priority order, added with `AddIntServer`)**.
4. VBlank server, a term of this chapter: a routine the operating system calls at every VBlank; the game has two, the sound effects engine's and its own `vblank_server`, which counts the VBlanks, takes the input samples and scrolls the ticker. Source: claim 3; `src/input.c`, the comment above `wof_vblank` ("the input half ... The ticker scroll of the second half"); chapter 2 ("counts the VBlanks, scrolls the ticker by one pixel, and every few VBlanks takes an input byte").
5. The logic tick: one step of the simulation, one for every input byte; the input byte is sampled every fourth VBlank into a queue of at most six, and the logic runs one tick per queued byte, so it runs at a quarter of the VBlank rate: 12.5 a second on PAL, 15 on NTSC. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, the bullets ("On every 4th VBlank it obtains one input byte and appends it to a FIFO of at most 6 entries"; "`run_queued_ticks` executes one logic tick per queued input byte"; "15 Hz on NTSC ..., 12.5 Hz on PAL"). Logic tick, input byte and input sample linked, not redefined (chapter 1).
6. The pass: one round of the inner loop, one drawn picture, as often as the machine manages and at most once a VBlank; on real hardware a pass takes longer than one VBlank. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, the inner loop ("one pass per displayed frame") and "Passes are not pure rendering" ("at most one pass per VBlank, counted from the previous buffer flip"); `SPEC.md` 6.2, Clock ("The original allows at most one pass per VBlank and on real hardware a pass takes longer than that"); `re/notes/passes.md`, "What the question is" ("a pass runs as often as the machine manages"). Pass linked, not redefined (chapter 1).
7. Why the logic takes its pace from the input bytes and not from the passes: the VBlank's interrupt fills the queue on time whatever the drawing costs, so a busy scene shows fewer pictures, not slower flight, and the game becomes a function of its inputs (chapter 2's reason, named, not retold). Source: chapter 2, "The VBlank" ("Because the interrupt fills the queue on time whatever the drawing costs, a busy scene shows fewer pictures, not slower flight"); `SPEC.md` 3.3 ("This design is what makes a verifiable port possible").
8. In the inner loop each pass calls `frame_update` (`0x010228`), a fixed pipeline of about twenty routines, and then `run_queued_ticks` (`0x0114D8`): first the picture, then the ticks. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, item 3 ("each pass then calls `frame_update` ..., a fixed pipeline of about twenty subsystem routines, ... and then `run_queued_ticks`"); `re/functions.csv`, `main`'s calls; `src/front.c`, `mission` (`CO_CALL(... wof_frame_update())`, then `run_queued_ticks()`).

## The input byte, every fourth VBlank

9. Every VBlank, unless the game is paused, the server calls `vblank_every_frame` (`0x01C9CA`), which times the fire button; then it counts a divider down and, when the divider runs out, sets it back to four and takes an input sample. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x011762`–`0x011780` (`tst.b pause_flag`; `jsr vblank_every_frame`; `subq.w #$1, vblank_divider`; `bgt.w $11842`; `move.w #$4, vblank_divider`); `re/notes/input.md`, "The chain".
10. The listing `vblank_server_divider`, `0x011774`–`0x011780`: the call of `vblank_every_frame`, the count down, the branch past the sample while the divider is above zero, the reload with 4. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x011774`–`0x011780`.
11. The divider begins at 0, so the first VBlank after a start samples: N VBlanks give N / 4 input samples rounded up. Source: `SPEC.md` 6.2, Clock ("The first VBlank after a start samples an input byte, because `vblank_server`'s divider begins at 0: N VBlanks give N / 4 samples rounded up"); `re/notes/headless.md`, "The fade's wait" (the divider `0x027362` "counts four and is never reset"). The prose keeps the first half, not the formula.
12. The sample: `read_joystick` (`0x01CA32`) assembles the input byte, the only channel by which the stick and the button reach the logic. Its low byte: bit 0 stick forward, bit 1 back, bit 2 right, bit 3 left, bit 4 fire held for 10 or more VBlanks, bit 5 fire tapped and released inside 10 VBlanks; bits 6 and 7 unused. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, "The input byte"; `re/notes/input.md`, "Input byte layout"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01CA32`–`0x01CAA0` (`bset.b #$5` on the tap latch, `#$4` on the hold latch, bits 0 to 3 from `read_joy_dispatch`, left and right swapped on the way). The prose names the six bits in words.
13. The directions are a level read at the sample: the stick's state at that VBlank; a push that begins and ends between two samples is not seen. Source: `re/notes/input.md`, "The chain" (`JOY1DAT` → `read_joy_bits` → `read_joystick`, every 4th VBlank); `SPEC.md` 6.2, Input ("the original samples a level"); chapter 1 ("A tap is kept until the next sample, so unlike a short push of the stick it is never lost").
14. The button is timed at VBlank rate: `vblank_every_frame` runs on every VBlank, notes the VBlank of the press, and sets a tap latch when the button comes up within 10 VBlanks of it, a hold latch when it is still down after 10; the latches persist until the next sample, which consumes and clears them; a tap wins, so one sample never reports both. Why VBlank rate and latches: a per-tick poll would lose a tap that starts and ends between two samples, and the threshold of 10 is VBlanks, not ticks. Source: `re/notes/input.md`, "Fire button and the tap/hold discrimination" (the C model, "Two consequences the port has to keep"); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01C9CA`–`0x01CA30` (`add.l #$a`, `fire_tap_latch`, `fire_hold_latch`); `SPEC.md` 3.3 ("The tap and hold timing runs at VBlank rate, not tick rate, and latches between samples").
15. Latch, a term of this chapter: a flag that keeps a brief event until it is read; the game latches a tap and a hold of the button between two input samples. Source: claim 14; `re/notes/input.md` ("tap / hold latches", "The latches persist between samples").
16. Why the button has two meanings: a tap drops the chosen weapon, a hold fires the guns (chapter 1, named). Source: chapter 1, "Logic, tick for tick"; `re/notes/passes.md`, "The instrument" (the other weapon dropped with a short click, the manual, page 7).
17. The queue: the byte goes to the end of `input_queue` (`0x027356`, its count at `0x027354`); when six are waiting, `input_queue_pop` first drops the oldest. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x0117D4`–`0x0117FA` (`jsr read_joystick`; `cmp.w #$6, d1`; `bcc.w` to `jsr input_queue_pop`; else `addq.w #$1, d1`; the count stored; the byte stored at `(count − 1) × 2`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x011714`, `input_queue_pop`'s header ("returns queue[0] in D0, shifts the queue"); `SPEC.md` 3.3 ("When the queue is full, the oldest entry is dropped").
18. The listing `vblank_server_queue`, `0x0117D4`–`0x0117FA`: the call of `read_joystick`, the test against 6, the pop of the oldest when full, the byte stored at the queue's end. Source: `re/Wings.lst`, the range.
19. Input queue, a term of this chapter: the list of up to six input bytes that the VBlank server fills and the logic ticks empty, one byte a tick, the oldest dropped when it is full. Source: claims 5, 17; `re/notes/input.md`, "The chain" (`input_queue 0x027356 (max 6)`).
20. Why a queue: a pass that takes longer than four VBlanks finds two or more bytes waiting and runs a tick for each, so the logic keeps its rate however long the pictures take. Source: chapter 2's figures box ("A pass longer than four VBlanks | finds 2 bytes, runs 2 ticks"); `SPEC.md` 3.3 (one tick per queued byte). Why the limit is six and not another number: no source (under "Unsourced").
21. In demo playback the server takes the byte from the recorded demo instead of the stick (`0x011786`–`0x0117D0`), and `run_queued_ticks` first waits until two samples have been taken, so a demo's pass has exactly two ticks; chapter 17 tells it. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, `demo_mode` ("A demo's pass waits for exactly two samples (`run_queued_ticks`, `0x0114E0`)"); `re/notes/headless.md`, "The fade's wait", consequences, last point; `re/Wings.lst` `0x0114D8`–`0x0114E4`.

## The tick follows the bytes

22. `run_queued_ticks` runs `logic_tick` (`0x011386`) while the queue's count is above zero; `logic_tick` pops one byte into `tick_input` (`0x026D42`), which about ten routines of the tick read. While the game is paused it returns without a tick. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x0114D8`–`0x01150E` (`tst.b pause_flag`, `bpl`, `rts`; the loop `jsr logic_tick` / `tst.w input_queue_count` / `bgt.b`); `re/notes/input.md`, "The chain" (`logic_tick 0x011386 pops it into tick_input 0x026D42`, "read by about ten subsystem routines"); `re/functions.csv` (`logic_tick` calls `input_queue_pop`).
23. The listing pair: `run_queued_ticks` from the listing, with its header, beside its port, `run_queued_ticks` in `src/front.c`, the same three steps: the demo's wait, the pause, one tick per queued byte. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x0114D8`–`0x01150E`; `src/front.c`, `run_queued_ticks` (`orig 0x0114D8`).
24. So the logic's rate is the input sample's, a quarter of the VBlank rate, whatever the pass rate, as long as the passes keep within the queue's six. Source: claims 5, 17, 20; `re/notes/headless.md`, "Scheduling" consequences ("a tick follows every second pass"); the scratchpad run `sched.py` (39 ticks after the mission's start at every rate of 1, 2 and 3; claim 49).

## A pass is not only a picture

25. `frame_update` is not a renderer: once per pass it runs game logic: the soldiers move and die, score is added, ticker messages are queued, objects are spawned, and the restart after a lost aircraft and the game-over countdown advance. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "What the question is"; `SPEC.md` 3.3, "Passes are not pure rendering".
26. Why that is no fault the port could correct: the port is a translation of what the code does (chapter 1), so per-pass logic stays per pass, and the pass rate becomes part of what the port must reproduce. Source: chapter 1 ("The port is not a reconstruction of what the game seems to do; it is a translation of what its code does"); `re/notes/passes.md`, "What the question is" ("the port has to know exactly which state crosses from one to the other").
27. Nine routines in a pass's call tree call `rand_beam`: a pass draws random numbers too. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3, "Passes are not pure rendering" ("Nine routines in its call tree call `rand_beam`"); `re/notes/passes.md`, "The control" ("a pass consumes entropy").
28. The instrument: every write and read of a headless run is tagged with its phase, `V` inside a VBlank's handlers and servers, `T` inside `logic_tick`'s tree, `F` inside `frame_update`'s tree, `M` in the main program outside them; `tools/pass_observe.py` runs a script twice, the first run saying which ranges a pass wrote, the second watching reads of exactly those ranges and saying who reads them inside a tick. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "The instrument"; `tools/pass_observe.py`, docstring and `observe`; chapter 6, "What a run leaves" (the write summary and the read hook, named).
29. 30. Over the seven runs a pass wrote 367 ranges, merged to 171 neighbouring blocks, and a tick read 62 of those blocks. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "The answer".
31. The couplings seen: the drawing copies `snapshot_for_draw` writes into every object record, which the logic reads back; the kind byte of an object record, which a pass clears or changes, so that the pass frees and changes object records; the pass counter, 0 to 99 (`0x0253C8`); the flag that a frame was drawn since the last tick (`0x026E3C`); the four pools of ricochets, splashes, smoke and balloons, which the pass spawns into; the soldier table, the soldiers living entirely in the pass; the blitter library's clip rectangle and drawing target and the double buffer's records. ## The schedule is an input

39. Therefore how passes and ticks interleave is an input of the simulation, like the input bytes and the entropy stream. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "What the question is" (in bold); `SPEC.md` 3.3 ("The interleaving of VBlanks, passes and ticks is therefore an input of the simulation, like the input bytes and the entropy stream"). The schedule is chapter 6's term, linked with a gloss.
40. The listing cannot say how many VBlanks a pass takes on a real machine, because that is processor time; the harness has no cycle model, and it delivers VBlanks only where the program waits, the pass's start owing the setting's VBlanks (chapter 6's "Scheduling", named, not retold). Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "What this does not answer" ("the harness has no cycle model"); `re/notes/headless.md`, "Scheduling"; chapter 6, "Time: only where the program waits".
41. How many VBlanks a pass takes sets the speed of the soldiers, of the game-over countdown, of the object animation and of everything else in the couplings. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "What this does not answer"; chapter 1 ("the soldiers, the game-over countdown and the objects' animation go by passes: a wrong count would change the game").
42. The control: the same script at one, two and three VBlanks per pass, over an entropy stream of one constant value (`{"entropy": {"constant": 10304}}`), so that the three runs see the same stream although a pass draws random numbers; compared at equal tick numbers, byte by byte. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "The control: one, two and three VBlanks per pass"; `tools/pass_observe.py`, docstring and `control`.
43. The comparison checks first that the three runs fed the tick the same input bytes, the schedule's own `T` entries, because otherwise it would say nothing; they were equal in every run. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "The control", first bullet.
44. Every differing byte falls into one of four classes: written by a pass; written only by a VBlank server or something it calls; written in a tick by a routine that reads a range a pass wrote; written in a tick by a routine such a reader calls. Anything else would be a finding. A server is recognised by its routine, not by the phase, because a VBlank can happen inside a tick. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "The control", the bullets; `tools/pass_observe.py`, docstring ("Whatever is left over is printed as a finding").

## Two VBlanks a pass: the film

51. Since no instrument of the project counts the processor's time, the owner filmed their PAL Amiga's monitor, from the machine's HDMI output, with a phone at 240 frames a second: the story scroller, and the hold with the lift, the flag and the aircraft accelerating on the deck. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, "What the film of the real machine shows", first paragraph; chapter 1 ("We filmed the machine, as no instrument of ours counts the processor's time"). No date in the prose.
52. The export plays the slowed section at 30 frames a second, so inside it one film frame is 1/240 s and a 50 Hz refresh is 4.8 film frames. Source: `re/notes/passes.md`, the same paragraph; `tools/film_rate.py`, docstring. ## How the port keeps time

61. The shell's clock is a fixed-rate accumulator driven by the browser's animation frames: it issues one VBlank of the core, `wof_vblank`, per 1/50 second of emulated time on PAL and 1/60 on NTSC, each followed by one `wof_pass`, independent of the monitor's refresh rate: a 144 Hz monitor and a 30 Hz one alike get 50 VBlanks a second on PAL, the second two to an animation frame. Source: `SPEC.md` 6.2, Clock; `web/clock.js`, the opening comment and `frame` (the `while (accumulator >= period)` loop calling `core.vblank(input.consume())` and `core.pass()`). Chapter 23 tells the clock in detail.
62. Why emulated time and not display time: the game's time is its VBlanks, so the clock counts the video standard's VBlanks, not the monitor's pictures. Source: `web/clock.js`, opening comment ("Emulated time, not display time, is what counts"; "because the original's inner loop runs one pass per displayed frame and the display is the VBlank").
63. `wof_vblank` is the port of `vblank_server` with `vblank_every_frame`: the flag the waits spin on, the pause, the divider, the sample, the queue of six. Source: `src/core.c`, opening comment; `src/input.c`, the comment above `wof_vblank` and its body (`if (count < 6) count++; else d0 = wof_input_queue_pop();`).
64. Why a pass entry after every VBlank and not after every second: `wof_pass` resumes the port's main program once; in the front end one wait is one VBlank, so the shell's pass after every VBlank lets the two agree VBlank for VBlank; in play the pass itself waits until its VBlanks have passed. Source: `SPEC.md` 6.3, second bullet ("In the front end one wait is one VBlank: the shell calls `wof_pass` after every `wof_vblank`, the headless original delivers a VBlank exactly where the program waits, and so the two agree VBlank for VBlank"); `src/core.c`, `wof_pass`.
65. The core counts the VBlanks a pass is owed: `wof_vblank` counts the VBlanks since the previous pass began, and `frame_update`'s port begins a pass only when the setting's number have passed and the VBlank flag is set, the headless original's rule; the setting is 2, a core setting with `wof_set_vblanks_per_pass`. VBlanks a tick waited, in the restart, count toward the next pass. Source: `src/world.c`, `wof_frame_update` (`CO_WAIT_UNTIL(c, wof_s.since_pass >= ... wof_vblanks_per_pass() && wof_g.vblank_flag)`) and its comment ("VBlanks inside a pass count toward the next one"); `src/input.c`, `wof_vblank` (`wof_s.since_pass++`); `src/core.c` (`vblanks_per_pass = 2`, `wof_set_vblanks_per_pass`); `SPEC.md` 6.2, Clock; `SPEC.md` 6.3, last bullet; `src/tick.c`, opening comment.
66. The port's tick can stop at the restart's waits and go on at the next VBlank, as the original's does: the path from `run_queued_ticks` down to that wait is a chain of coroutines, each `WaitTOF` one VBlank; chapter 22 tells how blocking code becomes coroutines. Source: `src/tick.c`, opening comment; `SPEC.md` 6.3, last bullet ("the path from `run_queued_ticks` through the player update down to the restart belongs to the coroutine").
67. After a stall, a page hidden or a slow machine, the clock replays at most 24 VBlanks, which matches the queue's limit: six entries, each worth four VBlanks; anything older would have been dropped on the machine as well. Source: `SPEC.md` 6.2, Clock ("After a stall, at most 24 VBlanks are replayed, which matches the original's queue limit of 6 ticks"); `web/clock.js` (`MAX_CATCHUP = 24` and its comment). The prose: "the six samples the queue would have held".
68. A key pressed and released between two VBlanks is held by the shell until the next VBlank, so a short tap survives the browser's coarser event timing; the original samples a level. Source: `SPEC.md` 6.2, Input. One clause or sheet only.
69. The program never checks the video rate: it reads neither `VBlankFrequency` nor `PowerSupplyFrequency` from ExecBase, nor `DisplayFlags` from GfxBase, and does not tell a 50 Hz machine from a 60 Hz one; on PAL everything runs at 50/4 ticks a second instead of 60/4, at five sixths of the speed. Source: `re/notes/random.md`, "Video rate"; `SPEC.md` 3.3 ("The program never checks the machine's video rate; at 50 Hz everything simply runs at five sixths of the speed"); `SPEC.md` 10, point 9. Chapter 1 stated the rates; this chapter gives the reason.
70. The game was designed for NTSC machines, where it takes 15 ticks a second. Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3 ("15 Hz on NTSC (the machine the game was designed for)"); chapter 1 ("the American machines the game was designed for").
71. The port's 50 Hz option is therefore nothing more than a different VBlank clock; one shell setting, PAL or NTSC, selects the VBlank rate and the pixel aspect together, and PAL is the default. Source: `re/notes/random.md`, "Video rate" ("The port's 50 Hz option is therefore nothing more than a different VBlank clock rate"); `SPEC.md` 6.2, Video ("Video standard"; "PAL is the default").
72. Only the music keeps time of its own, on a CIA timer, nearly the same on PAL and NTSC (chapter 2; chapter 18). Source: chapter 2, "Paula" ("The songs set it themselves, each its own tempo, nearly the same on PAL and NTSC"); `re/notes/music.md`, "The timer". One clause.
73. The figures box "PAL and NTSC": VBlanks a second 50 and 60; input samples and logic ticks a second 12.5 and 15; a pass in a quiet scene every 2nd VBlank on both (the port's setting; filmed on PAL). Source: claims 2, 5, 59, 65, 69. The box carries the two standards side by side; "five sixths" stays in the prose as the point (it is the source's own figure, not computed by the chapter).

## The fade step

74. A fade, a picture coming up from black or going down to it, is sixteen steps (step 0 to 15) of `colour_lerp` over every colour and a rebuild of the copper list, swapped in from the spare list, with no wait in it at all; a step lasts as long as the arithmetic and the rebuild take, so the fade's duration on the machine is processor time, which nothing in the executable gives. Source: `re/notes/display.md`, "Fades" ("**There is no wait inside the loop**: a step lasts as long as the arithmetic and the rebuild take, so the fade's duration is CPU time"); `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "The fades, and the one provisional setting"; `SPEC.md` 6.3, third bullet; `SPEC.md` 10, point 6; chapter 2, "The beam and the copper" (the sixteen rebuilds, the spare list).
75. The headless original's fades take no time, because it has no clock between wait points. Source: `re/notes/headless.md`, "The fade's wait", consequences ("Fades and other CPU-bound delays take no time"); `re/notes/frontend.md`, "The timetable, left alone" ("The fades take no time in the harness and their real duration is not established").
76. The port gives a fade step a fixed number of VBlanks, a core setting, 2 (`fade_vblanks` in `src/fade.c`, set with `wof_set_fade_vblanks`), set by the owner's eye on the publisher's picture, the title and the credits, and kept without a film; it is the one setting of time in the port that rests on no measurement. Source: `src/fade.c`, the opening comment and `fade_vblanks`; `SPEC.md` 6.3, third bullet ("kept by the owner's decision ... without a film of the real machine"); `CONTROLLER.md`, "Open items", "The duration of a fade step" ("The user's impression of the logo, title and credits decides it, and it is right for them: ... 2 stays and no film is made"); chapter 1 ("The port sets it to two VBlanks, and the fades were found right by eye"). "The one setting of time": the core has two settings of time, `wof_set_vblanks_per_pass` and `wof_set_fade_vblanks` (`src/wof.h`), and the other rests on the film.
77. The listing `fade_vblanks`: the static variable with its comment, "PROVISIONAL (SPEC 10, point 6) ... cannot be settled there; a cycle-exact emulator can". Source: `src/fade.c`, lines 18 to 20. Kind `c`, extended to file-scope variables (under "Listings").
78. The listing `wof_fade`: the body of the port's four fades, sixteen steps, each colouring, showing the view (what survives of the copper rebuild is that the VBlank flag is cleared), then waiting `fade_vblanks` VBlanks. Source: `src/fade.c`, `wof_fade` and the comment above it.
79. The comparisons set the fade step to 0, where the harness's fades take no time either, and the two then agree VBlank for VBlank. Source: `src/fade.c`, opening comment; `SPEC.md` 6.3 ("the differential tests set it to 0, as the harness's fades take no time either"); `tests/test_front_port.py`, `replay` (`fade_vblanks=0`); `tests/m4compare.py` (`ported.reset_core(fade_vblanks=0)`).
80. The fade's arithmetic itself is held to the original under the oracle (chapter 5's test); only its duration is the port's. Source: chapter 5 (`test_colour_lerp_matches_over_its_range`); `re/notes/display.md`, "Fades" ("This model agrees with the original under the oracle"). One clause.
81. The music's fade is a different wait, the front end asking the music player again and again until the song has faded; the harness and the port give each round four VBlanks to keep the input sample's phase (chapter 6, named; chapter 18). Source: `re/notes/headless.md`, "The fade's wait"; `SPEC.md` 6.3, fourth bullet. One clause, to keep the two fades apart.
