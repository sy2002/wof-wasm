## 1. What a shape is to the game

3. A container is loaded whole into chip memory and stays there in the file's own layout, unpacked: the game works on the file image. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, Summary ("loaded whole into chip memory and stays there unchanged in layout"); "Container and lookup" ("The container in memory is the unpacked file").
4. A shape, to the game, is a pointer to its record inside that image; a record is a header of 20 bytes followed by the shape's stored planes. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, Summary and "Record header, complete" (`+20` plane data); `SPEC.md` 3.5, the record.
5. The record of entry `i` lies at the container's start plus 6, plus 8 times the count, plus the entry's offset. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Container and lookup". In the sidebar or not at all; chapter 3 told the layout.
6. Why the game keeps the image: it reads a shape's fields and planes where they lie, so a pointer is all it needs. The chapter states what the game does, not an intent. Source: claims 3, 4; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete" ("No other reader exists": every routine takes a record from a pointer table or `shape_find` and reads `+0` to `+6`).
7. The twelve containers hold 1,049 shapes; eight of the files are packed, four are not (`battleship.shp`, `dash.shp`, `destroyer.shp`, `nightdash.shp`). Source: `book/docs/part-1/disk.md` (twelve containers, 1,049 shapes; ten packed files, eight of them containers); counted with `ppkc.parse` over `original/disk/Wings_of_Fury/shapes/*.shp` (counts table).
8. A shape stores 0 to 5 planes: one stores none, 85 one, 113 two, 336 three, 257 four, 257 five. Source: counted with `ppkc.parse` (counts table). In the sheet; the chapter says only that most store fewer than five.

## 2. The header, field by field

9. The header's fields, in order: `+0` the width in bytes (the pixel width is eight times it); `+2` the height in rows; `+4`, `+6` the hotspot, x and y, signed; `+8`, `+10` the position the shape was cut from in its source picture; `+12` the clear byte, `+13` the set byte; `+14` six bytes, the plane masks of the stored planes, ended by a zero; `+20` the planes. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete"; `SPEC.md` 3.5.
10. Figure `record-smk3`: the whole record of `smk3` of `world.shp`, the third of the smoke's frames, its header field by field and its three stored planes, each with its bits. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "The slots of the islands and the weapons" (`smk0` to `smk5`, smoke, by its kind); the bytes read by the figure's maker from the unpacked container.
11. `smk3`'s header: width 2 bytes, 16 pixels; height 8; hotspot (9, 4); cut position (80, 93); clear byte `0x04`; set byte 0; plane masks `01 02 18`. Its pixels are colours 25, 26 and 27. Source: scratch read with `rpck.load` and `ppkc.parse` (counts table), `world.shp` entry 110 at offset `0xC926` of the unpacked file; the figure's maker reads and checks the same bytes.
12. The hotspot is the point of the shape, counted from its top left corner, that lands on the position the shape is drawn at: the callers subtract it from the position before they draw. Why: a shape is placed by a point of its own, the smoke by its middle, the aircraft by a point of its body, so that the code that places it needs nothing of its size and frames of different sizes stay in place. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete" (`+4` "callers subtract it from the draw position"); `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines" (`draw_world_shape` subtracts the hotspot); `book/docs/part-1/disk.md` ("the point that lands on the position the shape is drawn at"). The reason is worded as what subtracting it does; "frames of different sizes stay in place" is the arithmetic of the subtraction, and the chapter shows it with `smk3`'s (9, 4) in a 16 by 8 box.
13. Listing `draw_world_shape` (`0x015174`): the world position turned into the screen's (chapter 13's), a position outside −128 to 448 left out, the slot times four, `movea.l (a0,d2.w),a0` loading the record's address from the pointer table, `sub.w $4(a0),d0` and `sub.w $6(a0),d1` taking off the hotspot, then `shape_draw` with A1 zero. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x015174`–`0x0151C0`; `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines" ("rejects x outside −128 to 448, subtracts the hotspot and calls `shape_draw`").
14. The words at `+8` and `+10` are the position the shape was cut from in its source picture; that is inferred from their values, multiples of 16 in x and many shapes of one animation sharing one position. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, Summary and "Open" (first point).
15. The game reads them in one place: the rank selection draws record number `rank` of `selectrank.shp` at that position, so the eight records carry the rows of the rank list: 64 and 91, 64 and 101, on to 64 and 161. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete" (the rank selection routine `0x018262`, `shape_draw_xor_c(record, word at +8, word at +10)` at `0x0182C6`, `0x018362`, `0x01838E`; "64/91, 64/101, 64/111, 64/121 and so on"); the eight values read with `ppkc.parse` (counts table).

17. The clear byte names the screen's planes set to 0, the set byte the planes set to 1, under the shape's mask, on every draw. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete" (`+12`, `+13`); `re/notes/drawing.md`, "What `shape_draw` does, exactly"; `SPEC.md` 3.5.
18. When both bytes are 0, the planes the shape does not store keep the background's bits, which is visible behaviour. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete", the paragraph "Values in the files" ("clear plane 4, so a 4-plane shape lands in colours 0 to 15 regardless of the background"; "keep the background's bits, which is visible behaviour").

## 3. From names to shapes

28. The game asks for its shapes by four-character names, through lists of names in its data; a name list is a zero-terminated list of names, one for each container it is resolved in. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, Summary; "Name lists and containers" ("The nine lists lie back to back at `0x023BC0`–`0x024628`, each ended by a zero long"); `book/docs/part-1/disk.md` (the names, the lists, a missing name an empty slot).
29. At load, `shapes_resolve` turns a list into a pointer table, an array of record pointers in the list's order, and the game's code then uses fixed numeric indices into it, the slots. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, Summary ("Game code then uses fixed numeric indices into these arrays"); "Container and lookup" (`shapes_resolve` `0x015C5C`: allocates 4 times the count, fills it with `shape_find` results).
30. Why resolve once: a lookup by name walks the names; a slot is one load. The code names a shape by its slot; the list and the container can change together without the code changing, which is how one list serves two containers (chapter 3). Source: `book/docs/part-1/disk.md` ("Names let one list be filled from different containers ... with the same code"); `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Name lists and containers" (`world_names` against `world.shp` and `8thscale.shp`; `dash_names` against `dash.shp` and `nightdash.shp`); claim 33 for the walk. The map's drawing skips a null slot, and a null record draws nothing elsewhere. ## 4. What is loaded when

42. The table: at start-up, once, `world.shp`, `hellcat.shp` (then the marker), `Torpedo.shp` (the marker), `japplane.shp`, `8thscale.shp`, in this order, kept until the program ends; at the rank selection, `selectrank.shp`, freed when the screen ends; before each mission, `dash.shp` or `nightdash.shp` by `night_flag`; after the map is loaded, each enemy ship's container only if the map holds that ship, by flags the map loader sets; at the end of a mission the dashboard's and the ships' containers freed; loading a saved game in a mission frees and repeats the mission's steps. Why: the five permanent containers hold what every mission draws, the world, the player's aircraft, its weapons, the enemy aircraft, the eighth scale; the rest is loaded only for the screen or the mission that draws it. Only the dashboard has night shapes; the world changes by palette alone. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "When containers are loaded and freed" (the table and the last paragraph: "Only the dashboard has night shapes"); the containers' contents from "Name lists and containers" and "The slots of the islands and the weapons". In the port both are unreachable, since the files are in the page. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "When containers are loaded and freed" ("Failure handling").

## 5. The blit, exactly

45. The mask rule of `shape_draw` (`0x020CE2`): a shape whose plane, its width in bytes times its height, is above 1,040 bytes gets no mask; otherwise a shape of two or more stored planes gets the OR of its planes, which the processor writes into `MaskBuffer`; a shape of one plane is its own mask, and `MaskBuffer` is not touched; a shape of no plane gets none; and the two callers of `shape_blit` itself, the dashboard's digits and a weapon marker, pass none. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "What `shape_draw` does, exactly", point 1; "The blitter library" (`MaskBuffer`, 1,040 bytes); `re/functions.csv` (`0x010344` `weapon_marker`, `0x01F2B0` `dash_digit`, callers of `shape_blit`).
46. Listing `shape_draw_mask` (`shape_draw` from `0x020CE2` to `0x020D26`): `cmpa.w #$0,a1` and the branch when a mask is given; `mulu.w $2(a0),d0`, the plane's size, compared with `MaskBuffer_size` and `bls.b`; the count of the plane masks in D1; `bhi.b` to the OR loops for two or more; for one, `lea.l $14(a0),a1`, the first plane as the mask; for none, `suba.l a1,a1`. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x020CE2`–`0x020D26`; `re/notes/drawing.md`, point 1.
47. The blit runs in three phases, one blit per plane each: first the clear planes, B the constant 0; then the set planes, B the constant `0xFFFF`; then for each stored plane, in stored order, the planes of its mask, B the stored plane. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "What `shape_draw` does, exactly", "In hardware terms"; `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, "The blit is per-pixel, and why".
48. Per written pixel, with M the target's depth mask, U the union of the stored planes' masks and p the shape's pixel, the three phases come to `v = (((v and not (clear and M)) or (set and M)) and not (U and M)) or (p and M)`; planes named by none of the clear byte, the set byte and U keep the background's bits. Source: `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, "The blit is per-pixel, and why" (the formula); `src/draw.c`, the opening comment.
49. A pixel is written at all only where the mask is set; without a mask the whole box is written and the shape is opaque, its colour-0 pixels over the background. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, point 2 ("such a shape is opaque").
50. The minterm `0xCA`, D = A·B + ¬A·C, with A the mask: where A is 1 the shape's bit, where A is 0 the screen's own. Without a mask, A is the constant `0xFFFF`. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "In hardware terms"; HRM 6 **(reference)** for the minterm as the low byte of `BLTCON0`, one bit per combination of A, B and C.
51. A minterm is the number that chooses the blitter's logic function: eight bits, one for each of the eight combinations of A, B and C, saying whether D is 1 there. Source: HRM 6 **(reference)**; `tests/blitter.py`, `minterm_word` ("D = the sum of the minterms LF names").
52. The eleven shapes above 1,040 bytes a plane, always opaque: `fcar`, `mcar`, `rcar` and `rank` in `world.shp`; `batb`, `batf`, `batm` in `battleship.shp`; `crus` in `cruiseship.shp`; `dstb`, `dstm` in `destroyer.shp`; `jrmt` in `japcarrier.shp`. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "What `shape_draw` does, exactly"; `SPEC.md` 3.5; checked with `ppkc.parse` (counts table). Chapter 2 counted them; the chapter names them.
53. The ten ship and carrier pieces hold no colour-0 pixel at all; nine carry the sky's colour 1 around the hull, and `mcar` fills its box and holds no colour 1 either; which shows that the artwork was made for this blit. `rank` is two-thirds colour 0 and is drawn on an empty screen, the briefing's. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, the paragraph after "In hardware terms"; counted with `to_indexed` (counts table: colour 0 in none of the ten, colour 1 in nine, `rank` 16,344 of 24,320 pixels colour 0). ## 6. Clipping to the pixel

60. A clip rectangle is the rectangle drawing is allowed in; `clip_set` (`0x02129C`) takes its top, bottom, left and right, the bottom and the right exclusive, and rounds the left and the right down to a multiple of 16 (`andi.w #$fff0`). Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The blitter library" (`clip_set`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x0212AC`, `0x0212B2`; `src/draw.c`, `wof_clip_set` ("a clip asked for at column 100 really clips at 96").
61. The rectangles the scene routines set, said as sizes (the controller's note 1): the playfield's 162 rows by 320 columns; the dashboard's 37 by 640; a window of the dashboard, rows 7 to 31 and columns 256 to 399 (the routine's 7, 32, 256, 400, the bottom and the right exclusive, which the sidebar says); one that keeps the playfield's sides and puts its bottom at a row computed from a ship's record (the water line), or 161; and the whole of the target for the front end's screens. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The blitter library" (`clip_playfield` `0x01524A`, `clip_dashboard` `0x01F2DC`, `clip_dash_window` `0x01525C`, `0x01526E`, `clip_set_full` `0x021280`); `re/functions.csv` (`0x01526E` `clip_to_waterline`; `clip_set_full`'s callers the rank selection, the briefing, the dialogs, the high scores). The record at `0x0254D8` is the player's carrier (`re/notes/enemy.md`, `ship_records`); the chapter says "the water line" as the routine's name does and claims no more.
62. Every left and right edge the game asks for is already a multiple of 16 (0, 320, 256, 400, 640, and the widths of its bitmaps). Source: claim 61, read from the table; the bitmaps' widths 320 and 640 (`book/docs/part-2/display.md`).
63. Why the rounding: the blitter works in words, 16 pixels of a plane, and with edges on word boundaries the routine skips whole words and masks the rest. Source: `src/draw.c`, `wof_clip_set` ("because the blitter works in words"); `re/notes/drawing.md`, point 3 ("Because left and right clip edges are multiples of 16, the routine gets there with whole-word skips plus first-word and last-word masks"); HRM 6 **(reference)** for a word of 16 bits.
64. The puzzle: a shape may lie at any x, the blitter writes whole words, yet clipping is exact to the pixel on all four sides, and so is the shape's own box. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, point 3; `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, "The blit is per-pixel, and why".
65. The answer: the destination span starts at x rounded down to a multiple of 16 and is one word wider than the shape when x is not a multiple of 16, so it overhangs the shape's box; the shifter moves the mask by x's remainder, shifting zeros in at the left; the first word mask, from `left_mask_table` by the clip's remainder, blanks the source columns left of the clip edge that the whole-word skip did not; the last word mask blanks the extra word on the right; and with minterm `0xCA` a 0 in A leaves the destination as it was. So every pixel of the shape's box inside the clip is written, and no other. The shifter carries across rows, and the last A word of every row is masked, so each row starts clean. Without a mask, A is the constant `0xFFFF`, and the same masks apply to it. Source: `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, "The blit is per-pixel, and why" (the argument, point by point); `src/draw.c`, the opening comment; `tests/blitter.py`, its docstring (the masks apply to A whether from memory or from `BLTADAT`); HRM 6 **(reference)** for the shifter and the word masks.
66. The first and last word masks are two registers of the blitter, ANDed with the first and the last word of every row of source A. Source: HRM 6 **(reference)**; `tests/blitter.py`, `execute` (`raw_a &= blit.afwm` on word 0, `&= blit.alwm` on the last).
67. The diagram `clip-blit.svg`: one row of a shape 32 pixels wide at x 37, the clip's left edge at 48; the words 32 to 47, 48 to 63, 64 to 79; the span of three words; the shift of 5; the first word mask `0x001F`, which keeps the last five columns of the first source word; the last word mask `0x0000`; the pixels written, 48 to 68. Source: the original's `shape_draw` under the oracle through `tests/blitcheck.py`'s `Reference` (counts table, the scratch run): `gun5` of `world.shp`, 32 by 6, at x 37 and clip `(0, 162, 48, 320)`: `fwm=001F lwm=0000`, three words a row, A shift 5 (`BLTCON0` `0x5FCA`); the written columns of a row whose mask fills the box follow from the masks (an opaque `fcar` at x 37 with the same clip, `fwm=001F lwm=0000`, changed columns 48 to 292, exactly its box from the clip on). The diagram's row is drawn with the mask set across the whole box.

## 8. The mirror

72. `load_permanent_shapes` stores 2 into `+8` of every record of `hellcat.shp` and `Torpedo.shp` (`0x012A1C`, `0x012A52`); 2 is the way the pixels face in the file. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x012A1C`, `0x012A52` (`move.w #$2, $8(a0)`).
73. `aircraft_frame` (`0x01ABDE`; state, facing, frame) looks the frame's shape up by name in both containers, compares `+8` with the facing plus 1, and when they differ stores the facing plus 1 and calls `shape_mirror_x`: the player's aircraft is mirrored in place, lazily, whenever the facing changes, and `+8` records which way the stored pixels face. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete"; `re/functions.csv` (`0x01ABDE` `aircraft_frame`, calls `shape_mirror_x`). Whole-book pass: the paragraph's first sentence, which said "the game keeps each frame once", now says that the game mirrors its frames of level flight and on the deck in place rather than keeping each twice, and that a turn's own frames are separate shapes, drawn as stored (chapter 14, "The stick", and its sheet, claims 39 to 42; `src/player.c`, `frame_select`), so that "once" stays with the mirroring. Follow-up of the pass: the mirrored frames are those of straight flight chosen by the pitch's target, climbs and dives among them, and those on the deck (chapter 14, "The stick"; `src/player.c`, `frame_select`); the sentence says "its frames of straight flight, climbing or diving, and those on the deck".
74. Why in place: the game keeps one copy of each frame and turns it round when the aircraft turns, once, not at every draw. Worded as what the game does; the marker is what makes it lazy. Source: claim 73. Memory as the reason is not claimed.
75. `shape_mirror_x` (`0x015B58`): every stored plane mirrored row by row, the bytes' order reversed and each byte's bits reversed through `bit_reverse_table` (`0x0256B6`); the hotspot's x becomes 8 times the width less 1 less the old x. It swaps bytes from both ends, so the middle byte of an odd byte width would stay unreversed; every width in the files is even. A null record returns at once. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete" (the paragraph on `shape_mirror_x`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x015B58`–`0x015BC4`; the widths checked with `ppkc.parse` (counts table: no odd width).
76. Why the hotspot turns too: the point that lands on the drawing position must stay the same point of the aircraft, now on the other side of its box. Source: claim 12 and the formula of claim 75 (8w − 1 − x is the same column counted from the right).
77. Listing pair: `shape_mirror_x` (`0x015B58`, with its header) beside `wof_shape_mirror_x` (`src/shapes.c`), which on indexed pixels is one row reversal, because a pixel carries the bits of all planes at once. Source: `re/Wings.lst`; `src/shapes.c`, the comment on `wof_shape_mirror_x`.
78. Figure `mirror-hc05`: the Hellcat `hc05`, 48 by 13, as stored and mirrored, its hotspot marked in both, (25, 8) and (22, 8). Source: `ppkc.parse` (hotspot 25, 8; width 6 bytes); the mirror computed by the maker on the stored planes by `shape_mirror_x`'s rule and checked against `to_indexed()`'s rows reversed and against the port's `wof_shape_mirror_x` through the native library (`wt_shape_mirror`, `wt_shape_field`), which is mirrored back after.
79. `aircraft_frame` does not test the lookup's result: for a frame name absent from one of the two containers it reads `+8` through a null pointer and may store to address 8, an exception vector nobody uses on the Amiga; the port needs a guard with the same visible result, none. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Names looked up at run time" (last paragraph); `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, "Open" (last point).

## 9. What the port made of it

80. The port has no planes and no blitter: each shape is converted once, at load, to indexed pixels, 8 times the width by the height, plus the header fields the blit still needs: the clear and set bytes, the union of the plane masks, the number of stored planes, the size of one plane, the hotspot and, for the two mirrored containers, the marker; then the file image is thrown away. Source: `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, Summary; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Consequences for the port"; `src/shapes.c`, the opening comment and `wof_shapes_load` ("the file image goes; the conversion stays"); `src/wof.h`, `wof_shape_t`; `SPEC.md` 6.4, first point.
81. A pointer table becomes an array of shape indices with −1 where the original holds a null pointer (`WOF_NO_SHAPE`); the slot numbers stay the interface to the game's code, and the tables are built from the lists in the executable, not from the files. Source: `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, Summary; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Consequences for the port" and "Name lists and containers" ("the port must build the tables from the lists in the executable"); `src/wof.h` (`#define WOF_NO_SHAPE ((int16_t)-1)`); `src/shapes.c`, `wof_shapes_resolve`.
82. Where the game keeps a pointer to a record in its state, the port keeps a handle, the container's number and the shape's index in one word, 0 for null, because a pointer cannot be part of a saved state of the core. Source: `src/shapes.c`, the comment above `wof_shape_handle` (SPEC 7.2). In the sidebar.
83. The mask is exactly "the pixel is not 0", because no container has overlapping plane masks, which the conversion checked over all 1,049 shapes; so it needs no storage. Source: `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, Summary and "What M1 did with this" in `re/notes/shapes.md`; `src/shapes.c`, the comment on `convert_record`.
84. The conversion follows the blit's plane loop rather than an OR, which with disjoint masks comes to the same. Source: `src/shapes.c`, the comment on `convert_record`. In the sidebar or not at all.
85. The blit works per pixel, with the clipping and the draw order identical to the original's; it is the one place the port cannot follow the original instruction by instruction. Source: `SPEC.md` 6.4, second point; `re/notes/porting-m1.md`, "The blit is per-pixel, and why" ("This is the one place where the port cannot follow the original instruction by instruction").
86. Listing `shape_blit_pixels` (a part of the static `shape_blit` in `src/draw.c`, from `uint8_t M` to the end of its loop): the bytes `keep` and `force` made from the clear and set bytes and the union, the mask as `p` not 0, one line that writes the pixel. Source: `src/draw.c`, `shape_blit` (`orig 0x020B0C together with 0x0209BC`). Needs the `c` kind to take a part by `from` and `to` (an extension of `book/tools/listings.py`).
87. The port's clip rounds as the original's does; `wof_shape_draw` decides the mask by the same two tests, a plane of at most 1,040 bytes and at least one stored plane; a null record returns first. Source: `src/draw.c`, `wof_clip_set`, `wof_shape_draw`.
88. The port mirrors its converted pixels by the same rule, and the hotspot with them; the shapes face the way the marker says, which the comparisons of whole runs carry across. Source: `src/shapes.c`, `wof_shape_mirror_x`, `wof_shape_set_facing`; `book/docs/part-1/mission.md` ("which way the shapes that the tick mirrors in place are facing", in both loops).
89. The port's `shape_by_index` refuses an index outside the container, where the original reads garbage. Source: `src/shapes.c`, `wof_shape_by_index`.

## Terms and glossary entries

Introduced in chapter 12, in bold with an entry; no earlier chapter links or bolds any of them, so the build gives each "First met and defined in chapter 12" (chapter 3 says "hotspot" and chapter 5 "the programme" in plain text, which the build does not count):

- Shape record: one shape inside a container, a header of 20 bytes and the planes it stores; to the game a shape is a pointer to its record. The detail: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Record header, complete"; `SPEC.md` 3.5.
- Hotspot: the point of a shape, from its top left corner, that lands on the position it is drawn at. The detail: the same.
- Clear and set bytes (the heading as the chapter speaks of them; the sheet first said "Clear and set planes"): the two bytes of a shape's header naming the screen's planes set to 0 and to 1 under its mask on every draw. The detail: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "What `shape_draw` does, exactly".
- Plane mask: the byte that names the screen's planes one stored plane of a shape is written to. The detail: `SPEC.md` 3.5.
- Name list: a list of four-character shape names in the program's data, ended by a zero, resolved in a container at load. The detail: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "Name lists and containers".
- Pointer table: the array of record pointers a name list becomes at load, in the list's order, null where a name is absent. The detail: the same, Summary.
- Slot: a fixed position in a pointer table, by which the code and the map's records name a shape. The detail: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "MasterList and AthList".
- Opaque shape: a shape drawn without a mask, its whole box written, colour 0 included. The detail: `re/notes/drawing.md`.
