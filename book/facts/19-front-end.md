# Fact sheet: chapter 19, The front end and the keys

Every claim the chapter makes, one line each, with its source, in the order of the chapter. A claim without a source goes to the list at the end and stays out of the draft. A general fact that the notes do not state is sourced in a reference work and marked **(reference)**; what the game does always comes from the notes, the listing and the port's sources. The port and the instruments are gathered at the chapter's end, as the opening says; no "How we know" box and no "What went wrong" box (`book/BOOK.md` 4, point 6; the controller's task).

Counts are of the branch's base, `63ed33b`, counted in the files named, by reading or with the command in the counts table at the end. Run for a fact, each on one core: one headless run of the idle front end, `tests/runs/front-end-idle.json` through `test_front_port.headless_run` with observers on eleven routines (7.9 seconds), for the timetable's stations; `extract_tables.Image` on the executable for the small tables and the texts' lengths; the generated `src/gen/tables.c` for the key table's size and its characters; the native library through `book/tools/figures.py`'s `run_core` (under a second a run) for the three screens and the VBlank at which the rank menu first reads the stick. No test run, no browser, no rebuild of the native library.

Words used in the sheet as the chapter will use them. A **key** is a key of the keyboard; a **raw key code** (or "code") is the number the keyboard sends for a key's position; the **qualifier** is the word of bits beside it (Shift, Caps Lock, Control, Alt, Amiga), never "modifier". A **round** is one round of a wait of the front end, one `graphics.WaitTOF`, one VBlank (`re/notes/frontend.md`, "The timetable, left alone"); the music's waits are counted in VBlanks, never in rounds. A menu's line is an **item** (chapter 17's word: "the load item"); the dialog's six name boxes are its **slots**, never a shape's slot, which is chapter 12's; a line of the high-score table is a **row** (chapter 17's); an **entry** is only the directory's, what `ExNext` hands out. The **window** is the story scroller's visible rows, never a browser's. A **view** is the display's (chapter 11); a **pen** is graphics.library's colour number for drawing; **the flip** is the vertical flip. A **fade** is the display's sixteen steps (chapter 11); the music's is **the song's fade** (chapter 18). A **reader** is one of the five routines that take keys out of the buffer. **Step S** is chapter 8's, the mission's first tick after its setup.

## 2. The story scroller

14. **Story scroller**, a term of this chapter: the screen that scrolls the game's story up the screen, line by line, from the program's start, behind song 2. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md`, "The story scroller — `story_screen` `0x017E80`"; `src/front.c`, `story_screen`.
15. The crack's own text screen, which comes before it in this executable, is left out of the port (chapter 1). Source: `SPEC.md` 3.1; `book/docs/part-1/faithful.md`, "What was left out"; `re/notes/frontend.md`, the state diagram (`crack_text_screen`). A clause.
16. The screen: one view of 640 x 200 and one plane, 230 rows shown from display line 5. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md` (Screen); `src/screen.c`, `wof_screen_story` (`width 640`, `height 200`, `depth 1`, `out_y 5`, `disp_rows 0xE6`).
17. The bitmap is a ring: each step, two `wait_frames_or_fire(2)`, four VBlanks, advances the plane's start by 80 bytes, one row, and a count from `0xD2` (210) down to 0 sends it back to the first row; the copper reloads the plane's address at the row where the bitmap ends, so the window wraps round. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md` (Per step); `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "The story scroller's ring and its ramps"; `book/docs/part-2/display.md` ("a ring that chapter 19 tells").
18. Why a ring: 200 rows of bitmap fill 230 lines of the window, and the text moves a row a step without anything being copied. *(What the code does; the plane pointer advances, no copy.)* Source: claim 17; `re/notes/frontend.md` (Per step: only `Planes[0]` advances). No design reason claimed.
19. Every fourteenth step, every 56 VBlanks, a band of 12 rows is cleared with `RectFill` in pen 0 and the next line is drawn in pen 1 in the game's font. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md` (Every 14th step); `src/front.c`, `story_screen`.
20. The lines are a block of strings in the executable, at `0x017494`, 2,152 bytes up to `0x017CFB`, 54 lines of which the scroller draws 53, the empty one that ends the last paragraph not; the port reads the block at build time. Source: `re/tables.toml`, `story_text` (the comment and `count = 2152`); `re/notes/frontend.md` (Loads; 53 lines, `line_count < 0x35`). Named by address and length, never quoted.
21. A line is drawn justified to 615 pixels (`0x267`) through `text_draw_justified` (`0x015A8C`): `text_render` (`0x015956`) draws the line by the processor into a one-bit template of 640 x 12, `MaskBuffer`, and `graphics.BltTemplate` puts the template on the screen with the RastPort's pen and draw mode. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "Text" (the table); `re/notes/frontend.md` (Every 14th step).
22. Justification: the surplus over the text's width is spread over the gaps between characters, the quotient to every gap and one more pixel to the first remainder gaps. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "Text" (`text_render`); `tests/test_oracle_m3.py`, `test_the_justified_template_agrees_with_the_original` (docstring).
23. The last line of a paragraph is not stretched: the width is 615 when the byte after the line's terminator is not 0, and 0 when it is, the empty line that follows. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md` (Every 14th step); `src/front.c` (`line[len + 1] ? 0x267 : 0`).
24. The first line is drawn at row 196 (`0xC4`), every later one at `0xC4 - 0xD2`, 14 rows before the plane's current start, which because of the ring is the bottom of the window: the drawing writes before the plane. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md` (Every 14th step); `src/front.c` (`0xC4 - 0xD2`); `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "graphics.library on indexed pixels" (It does not clip).
25. On the machine that write lands in the rest of the view's own memory block, because the RastPort has no Layer and graphics.library does not clip there; the port's graphics layer must not clip either, and what bounds a write is the view's block, as on the machine. Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "graphics.library on indexed pixels"; `src/gfx.c`, opening comment and `block_of`.
26. Why the port's view block is 640 x 260 bytes and not the machine's `0xACD0`: the plane advances 210 rows and the window shows 230 from wherever it starts, so the display reads up to row 258 of the view's memory and the text is drawn down to row 209; on the machine those rows are the cleared rest of the block, black in the port for the same reason. Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "Views, viewports and what reaches the output"; `src/wof.h` (`WOF_VIEW_ROWS 260`). Chapter 11's box gave the 640 x 260: linked.
27. The two grey ramps: the copper list sets colour 1 to `i x 0x111` at viewport row i for the 16 rows at the top and again at row 196 - i at the bottom, the brightest between them, black below, so the text fades in at the bottom and out at the top. Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "The story scroller's ring and its ramps"; `tests/test_oracle_m3.py`, `test_the_scrollers_ramps_are_the_grey_ramp_of_the_copper_builder`.
28. The port gives each distinct value a palette of its own: seventeen, the sixteen greys and black, which is why it keeps 24 palettes (chapter 11). Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md` (ibid.; `WOF_PAL_COUNT` raised to 24); `book/docs/part-2/display.md` (the seventeen); `src/wof.h` (`WOF_PAL_COUNT 24`). Linked.
29. It ends on fire, or 210 steps after the last line was drawn, with a wind-down of 16 rounds of two VBlanks that takes both ramps out; no key is read, so a key pressed then waits in the buffer until the rank selection. Source: `re/notes/frontend.md` (Ends; Reads no key); `tests/test_frontend.py`, `test_the_story_scroller_reads_no_key` (`KEY_COUNT == 1`, run `scroller-key`).
30. Figure (a), new, `story-scroller.png`, maker `screen`, run `story` (no input), VBlank 780: the story's heading dimmed in the top ramp, the first paragraph white, its last line unstretched and dimmed in the bottom ramp. Why VBlank 780: the first lines have risen to the top of the window, so both ramps carry text; earlier only the bottom ramp does. Source: `book/figures.toml`; the scratch renders at VBlanks 400, 700, 760, 780, 1000 (counts table).
31. The port: `story_screen` in `src/front.c` with the ring as one field, `ring_at`, where the band builder splits the run; the reload only for a wrap row of 196 or less, the three places of `story_copper_build` covering 0 to 15, 16 to 180 and 181 to 196. ## 9. How a key reaches the game

91. Why a path of its own: the keyboard never reaches the logic through the input byte; the commands come in through the game's own key handler (chapter 1). Source: `re/notes/input.md`, "Keyboard and mouse" ("Entirely separate from the input byte"); `book/docs/part-1/faithful.md` (the input byte paragraph). Linked.
92. `input_handler` (`0x02075A`) is installed by the game on input.device at priority 127; for each event of the raw key class it ignores a key going up (bit 7 of the code set), appends the **raw key code** to `key_buffer` (`0x026C98`) and the **qualifier** word to `key_qualifier_buffer` (`0x026CA2`) and clears the event's class, so that the event never reaches the rest of the system. Source: `re/notes/keys.md`, "The chain from a key press to an effect"; `re/notes/input.md`, "Keyboard and mouse"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x02075A`–`0x0207D6`.
93. Why priority 127 and the swallowed event: a handler of the highest priority sees an event before the system's own handlers, and an event whose class is cleared reaches none of them. *ROM Kernel Reference Manual: Libraries and Devices*, input.device **(reference)**; what the game does: claim 92.
94. **Raw key code**, a term of this chapter: the number from 0 to 127 the keyboard sends for a key's position, with bit 7 set when the key goes up; it names where the key is, not what is printed on it. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` ("Raw codes are positional"); `re/notes/input.md`, "Keyboard and mouse"; `tools/extract_tables.py` (`KEYMAP_CODES = 128`, the comment: `input_handler` drops a code with bit 7 set, a key going up). Chapter 2's sentence, linked.
95. **Qualifier**, a term of this chapter: the word of bits that comes with a raw key code and says which of Shift, Caps Lock, Control, Alt and the Amiga keys were held. Source: `re/notes/keys.md`, "Control, and the two masks"; `tools/extract_tables.py` (`lshift 1, rshift 2, capslock 4, ctrl 8`); ROM Kernel Reference Manual **(reference)** for the bits' names.
96. The qualifier's bits as the game and the port use them: `0x0001` left Shift, `0x0002` right Shift, `0x0004` Caps Lock, `0x0008` Control, `0x0010` left Alt, `0x0080` right Amiga. Source: `tools/extract_tables.py` (the first four); `re/notes/keys.md` (`0x10`, left Alt; right Amiga for the clear); `src/dialog.c`, `wof_text_input` (`qualifier & 0x0080`); ROM Kernel Reference Manual **(reference)**.
97. **Key buffer**, a term of this chapter: ten raw key codes and ten qualifier words with a count, `key_count` (`0x026CB6`), filled by the handler and emptied by the readers; a key that does not fit is dropped. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (the chain; "What the port has to keep": "The buffer is 10 deep and drops what does not fit"); `src/keys.c`, `wof_key`.
98. `key_available` (`0x0207D8`) answers `0xFF` when a key is waiting; `key_get` (`0x0207E4`) pops the oldest as the qualifier in the upper word and the code in the lower, and shifts the buffer down. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (the chain); `re/Wings.lst` `0x0207D8`–`0x020846`.
99. `key_get` waits with `WaitTOF` while the buffer is empty; only `key_wait_char` (`0x0206EE`) would reach it without asking first, and it has no caller. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (after the chain); `re/Wings.lst` (`key_wait_char`: "No caller").
100. Because raw codes are positional, the port's map is positional too: a German keyboard gives the same codes as an American one. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (Raw codes are positional; For the port); `web/input.js` (the comment on `RAW_CODES`).
101. The off-by-one: `key_get`'s shift loop runs from 0 up to and including the new `key_count`, so with a full buffer its last round copies one entry past the end of each array: on the machine the byte after `key_buffer`'s ten is the first byte of `key_qualifier_buffer`, the high byte of its first word, which by then holds what the second held, and the word after the ten qualifiers is `key_count` itself. Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "The off-by-one that is state"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x020818`–`0x02082C` (`cmp.w ... key_count`, `ble.b`); `tests/test_oracle_m3.py`, `test_popping_a_full_buffer_reproduces_the_original_off_by_one`.
102. Nothing reads those slots until the buffer fills again, but they are state, compared with the original's, so the port has to leave the same values behind; it keeps the values rather than the adjacency, so the quirk survives a struct whose member order is the port's own (`key_code_at`, `key_qualifier_at`). The chapter's first why. Source: `tests/test_oracle_m3.py` (the test's docstring); `src/keys.c` (the comment above `key_code_at`); `re/notes/porting-m3.md` (ibid.).
103. Listing pair: `key_get`'s pop and shift (asm, `0x0207F2`–`0x02082C`) beside `key_code_at` (c, `src/keys.c`, with the comment above it, which names `key_qualifier_at` too).
104. **Control** as a qualifier is the one bit the game reads, `0x0008`, tested as `and.w #8` by `ingame_keys` at `0x01CD24` and as `btst #3` by the briefing at `0x018712`. Source: `re/notes/keys.md`, "Control, and the two masks"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01CD24`, `0x018712`. (The glossary's Control is chapter 8's, a change made on purpose: the chapter writes "the Control key" and "the Control bit" and never links Control.)
105. The Control key's own raw code, `0x63`, is buffered like any other key and converts to no character, so it matches no command. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (ibid.); `tests/test_frontend.py`, `test_the_control_key_may_come_as_its_own_event_too`.
106. The qualifier mask, `key_qualifier_mask` (`0x026D30`), is set once by `keyboard_open` (`0x0205CC`) from `key_mask_default` (`0x027162`), which is 0 unless `task_setup` (`0x0125C6`) finds a trap handler other than the one dos gives a process, when it becomes `0x10`, left Alt, and every key must then be pressed with left Alt; a game started from the CLI under Kickstart 1.3 takes the first branch, so the mask is 0 in play, dead. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (ibid.); `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "What the port keeps". No reason for the branch claimed.
107. `key_to_char` (`0x020700`) turns a key into a character through console.device's `RawKeyConvert` with a buffer of one byte and the system's default keymap, and takes the character only when exactly one comes back: a cursor or function key gives two or three and so none. Source: `re/notes/keys.md`, "Raw code to character: console.device"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x020700`–`0x020758` (`pea.l $1.w`, `cmp.w #$1, d0`).
108. **Keymap**, a term of this chapter: the table by which the system turns a raw key code and its qualifier into characters; the system's default keymap lies in the Kickstart ROM, not on the game's disk. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (ibid.: "depends on the system keymap, which is not on the game disk"); ROM Kernel Reference Manual, console.device and the keymap **(reference)**.
109. Table in the chapter, ten conversions observed under the harness: `0x13` gives `r`, with left Shift `R`, with Control `0x12`; `0x45` (Escape) `0x1B`; `0x44` (Return) `0x0D`; `0x41` (Backspace) `0x08`; `0x46` (Delete) `0x7F`; `0x40` a space; `0x4C` (cursor up) two characters, so none for the game; `0x50` (F1) three, so none. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (the table); `tests/test_frontend.py`, `test_the_rom_converts_raw_codes_as_the_game_expects`; `src/gen/tables.c` (counts table: the same ten from the port's table).
110. The harness runs the ROM's own `RawKeyConvert` and keymap, found in `original/kick.rom` by their contents, as the floating point and topaz 8 are: console.device's resident by its name at `0xFE4C6C`, `RawKeyConvert` at `0xFE6D18` through its function table, the keymap at `0xFE7F8A` by a pointer to its table of the QWERTY row. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (the table "What / Where / Found by"); `re/notes/headless.md`, "The keyboard needs the ROM too"; `book/docs/part-1/headless.md` (chapter 6: the floating point and `RawKeyConvert` run from the ROM). Linked to chapters 2 and 6.
111. The port's table: the build runs the ROM's own routine with the ROM's default keymap over every raw code from `0x00` to `0x7F` under the sixteen combinations of four qualifier bits, the two Shifts, Caps Lock and Control, and writes the single characters: 2,048 bytes, 1,050 of them characters. Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "The key conversion table"; `tools/extract_tables.py`, `keymap_from_rom` (docstring), `KEYMAP_CODES = 128`, `KEYMAP_QUALIFIERS = 16`; `src/gen/tables.c` (counts table: 2,048 entries, 1,050 not 0).
112. Why four bits: the shell ignores a key pressed with Alt or Command and never sends those bits, and the game itself tests only Control; right Amiga's clear is tested before any conversion. Source: `re/notes/porting-m3.md`, "The key conversion table"; `src/keys.c`, `wof_key_to_char` (comment).
113. Why the ROM's own routine rather than one written anew: the routine's rules (a type byte per key choosing which of four bytes applies, Control and-ing the result with `0x9F`, string and dead-key entries) would have to be rewritten, which buys nothing, because the game never converts with a keymap of its own and never asks for more than one character; running the ROM makes the ROM the reference. The chapter's second why. Source: `re/notes/keys.md` (For the port: "Reimplementing the routine instead ... buys nothing here"); `book/docs/part-1/headless.md` ("running them makes the ROM itself ... the reference").
114. The keymap is the system's, not the game's, so the build takes it from the owner's ROM, which is not in the repository; the build stops without the ROM, and `tools/extract_tables.py` run alone without it falls back to what the codes' positions give: letters, digits and the space bar, Return, Escape, Backspace, Delete and the cursor keys still working because the readers test them by their raw code before any conversion. Source: `SPEC.md` 5, step 1 (the ROM check; the fallback "for a run of it alone, which the build never reaches"); `tools/extract_tables.py` (`POSITIONAL`, `keymap_positional`, the comment above it); `re/notes/system-font.md`, "For the build".

Figure (e), new, hand-drawn, `key-path.svg`: on the left the original's path, a key, the handler on input.device at priority 127, the buffer of ten codes and ten qualifier words, the five readers, and `key_to_char` through `RawKeyConvert` and the ROM's keymap; on the right the port's, a key's `KeyboardEvent.code`, the shell's map to raw codes, the key layer, `wof_key` into the same buffer, the same five readers, and the table of 2,048 bytes the build made by running the ROM's routine.
