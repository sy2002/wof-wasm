# Fact sheet: chapter 15, Weapons, targets and soldiers

Every claim the chapter makes, one line each, with its source, in the order of the chapter. A claim without a source goes to the list at the end and stays out of the draft. A general fact about the world that the notes do not state is sourced in a reference work and marked **(reference)**; what the game does always comes from the notes, the listing and the port's sources. Each mechanism ends with what the port made of it and, in one plain sentence, the instrument of Part I that holds it, or the chapter says that the port and the instruments are gathered at its end; no "How we know" box (`book/BOOK.md` 4, point 6; the controller's task).

Counts are of the branch's base, `13b423e`, counted in the files named, by reading or with the command in the counts table at the end. Run for a fact, each on one core: the headless original over the script `rockets_a` with the state read after every pass (10 seconds), which also kept the run's schedule; the native library replaying that schedule (under a second); `extract_tables.Image` for the small tables of the executable; `ppkc.parse` for the shapes' names and sizes; a Python mirror of `object_step`'s arithmetic (scratch) for the diagram's dots. No test run, no browser, no rebuild of the native library.

Words used in the sheet as the chapter will use them. An **object record** is one of the fifteen records of `object_records` (and the sixteenth); a **map record** is chapter 13's word of the map. "Slot" is a shape's slot only (chapter 12). A soldier's **state** and a record's **kind** are the game's words; the oracle's random inputs are "random settings of the state" or "random states", as chapters 5 and 14 say. A **hit** is a weapon's impact on what lies under it (`weapon_hit`); the targets' fire hitting the aircraft is "a hit on the aircraft", and its count is "the hit count". **Height** is world y, pixels above the water line (chapter 13); **east** is +1, the larger world x. A **pass** and a **tick** are chapter 7's.

## 2. Fifteen records and a byte

8. Every weapon in flight is an **object record**: one of fifteen records of `0x2A` bytes, 42, at `object_records` (`0x024CAE`), with a sixteenth at `0x025594`. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The inventory".
9. The sixteenth is where an enemy torpedo bomber's torpedo runs (chapter 16). Source: `re/notes/enemy.md` (line 129: "drops its torpedo into `object_record_extra`"); `src/enemy.c`.
10. There is no allocator: every table carries a byte or word that says whether a record is in use, and a claim walks the table for the first free one. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "Claiming and freeing a record".
11. An object record's byte at `+0x20`, its kind, says in use: `0xFF` while it flies, 8 while it goes out, 0 when free. Its word at `+0x22` is its type, the weapon: 0 a rocket, 1 a bomb, 2 the torpedo, the `weapon_type` at the drop. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "What decides a record's behaviour" and "Record layout"; `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "How `+0x12` moves `+0x04`, and what `+0x22` is" (observed over the scripts).
12. A tap of the button in the air, unless the aircraft is inside a turn (its attitude from 6 to 16), drops the weapon (`0x01B5E2`, `weapon_drop` `0x01107C`); held, the button fires the guns at attitude 0. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the drop"; `src/player.c`, `button` (`0x01B5B0`); `book/docs/part-2/player.md` (told there; linked).
13. The drop's walk (`object_draw_first`, `0x0107F2`): with no weapon left it does nothing; else it tests the kind byte of the fifteen records in turn (`moveq #$e` and `dbra`, fifteen rounds) and gives the first free one to the launch (`0x01088E`); never the sixteenth. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x0107F2`–`0x01081E`; `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the drop"; `src/objects.c`, `object_draw_first`.
14. With all fifteen in use the walk returns: the shot is silently dropped, and since the weapon is taken only in the launch, it is not counted. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "Claiming and freeing a record" ("the game silently drops a shot when its table is full"); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01081A` (the return) and `0x01088E`–`0x010898` (`subq.b #$1` of `weapon_count` in the launch); `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Appendix: the regions no run executed" (`0x01081A`: no weapon left or every record in use, nothing is dropped). *(Reasoning from the listing for "not counted", marked.)*
15. `object_spawn` (`0x010820`), the same walk, leaves the explosions of a crash or a wreck at a map record, kind 8, type 1. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "Claiming and freeing a record"; `re/notes/porting-m5.md` ("`object_spawn`'s first entry, the explosion of a crash or a landing on the water, makes type 1 with the kind 8"); `src/objects.c`, `wof_object_spawn`. A clause.
16. The launch: one weapon less unless unlimited; the type; the aircraft's two speeds and its x (the horizontal speed turned by the facing), its height at the aircraft's place; a bomb or the torpedo is in flight at once. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the drop"; `src/objects.c`, `launch` (`0x01088E`); `src/tick.c`, `shot_origin` (`0x011274`).
17. Listing pair: `object_draw_first` (asm, whole, `0x0107F2`–`0x01081E`) beside `object_draw_first` (c, `src/objects.c`). Source: `re/Wings.lst`; `src/objects.c`.
18. A record is freed by clearing its kind byte: in the tick when a rocket leaves its reach, in the pass after eight frames of going out. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "Claiming and freeing a record" (`clr.b +0x20` in `0x010AA6` and in `0x010702`).
19. The pass draws the records (`0x010702`) and frees those that have gone out; the tick moves them (`0x010A72`). A record freed in a pass is free for the next tick's drop: a coupling of chapter 7 ("the kind byte a pass clears there, which frees the record"). Source: `SPEC.md` 3.3; `book/docs/part-1/time.md`, the couplings' table.

## 3. A weapon in flight

20. `objects_step` (`0x010A72`) walks the fifteen records and the sixteenth once a tick and hands each in use to `object_step` (`0x010AA6`). Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "What decides a record's behaviour", "The order of a tick"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x010A72`–`0x010AA4`.
21. What a record does is decided by a chain of tests, not a table of routines or a switch: the kind against 8 (going out: nothing to do), the type against 0 (a rocket), then 2 and the frame byte against `0x0A` (a torpedo running in the sea); everything else falls like a bomb. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "What decides a record's behaviour"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x010AAC`–`0x010B54`.
22. Listing: `object_step` from `0x010AA6` to `0x010AF8` (kind `asm`, as `object_step_head`), the head of the chain and the rocket's fall, aim and thrust. Source: `re/Wings.lst`.
23. A record keeps its position and its speeds as longs of 16.16 fixed point, the whole pixels in the upper word. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "Record layout" (the table: `+0x00` x, `+0x04` y, `+0x0E` and `+0x12` the speeds).
24. A bomb, and the torpedo while it falls, loses a tenth of the whole part of its horizontal speed each tick (`divs.w #$a`, truncated, so below ten pixels a tick nothing), and gravity, `0x6000` at `0x025350`, is taken off its vertical speed each tick; the height moves by the whole part. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight" and "How `+0x12` moves `+0x04`"; `re/notes/objects.md`, "Record layout" (three eighths of a pixel per tick); `re/Wings.lst` `0x010B58`–`0x010B86`.
25. `0x6000` is three eighths of a pixel in 16.16: `0x6000` over `0x10000` is 0.375. *(Derived.)* Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "Record layout".
26. The height's fraction never carries: the tick writes back only the whole word of the height and its fraction at `+0x06` stays 0, so the height moves by the whole part of the speed, rounded down; `tools/m5_observe.py fall` found the rule held in all 411 ticks of bombs and torpedoes it checked. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "How `+0x12` moves `+0x04`" (observed, 411 cases); `re/notes/objects.md`, "The fall". Sidebar or sheet.
27. A bomb's frame steps on every other pass that drew it, round its twelve frames, `bom0` to `bomb` (`torpedo_shapes` `0x40` on). Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight" (last point) and "The object records in the pass"; `src/objects.c`, `object_draw`; names counted (counts table).
28. A rocket also takes, at the launch: half the bearing (`+0x26`), the airspeed (`+0x28`), a thrust of twice the bearing's sine and cosine (`+0x1A`, `+0x16`, the cosine turned with the speed's sign), a fall of 4, 8 or 12 ticks (`+0x24`), and a frame from the bearing, 0 to 9, ten more flying west. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the drop"; `src/objects.c`, `launch`.
29. The bearing (`0x025404`) is the aircraft's pitch as an angle of the game's sine tables, worked out by `shot_origin` each tick for what the aircraft shoots or drops next: `pitch × 2 × 0x200 / 0x4650`; its half is the angle the sine takes, a turn being `0x400`. Source: `src/tick.c`, `shot_origin` (`0x011274`) and `wof_sine` (`0x015108`, "0x400 steps a turn"). *(The units: reasoning from the arithmetic, marked; the chapter says only "the aircraft's pitch as an angle".)*
30. The fall: bits 2 and 3 of the last of four draws of the beam (after `rol.w #1`), 0 counting as 8: 4, 8 or 12. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the drop"; `src/objects.c`, `launch`.
31. While the fall counts, the rocket moves a pixel back (against the player's facing) and a pixel down each tick besides its speed; the tick the count reaches 0 it is aimed, and from then on its thrust adds to its speeds every tick. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x010AC0`–`0x010AF8`; `src/objects.c`, `object_step`.
32. Fired level, a rocket falls the count's pixels and then flies on level, faster each tick; it comes down within reach only from a dive. Source: `tools/m5_autopilot.py`, the comment on the plan `rockets_a` ("a rocket falls for a few ticks, then flies on along the aircraft's pitch, accelerating ... so it comes down within reach only from a dive"); the counts table, the diagram's dots (the level rocket: the sine of 0 is 0, no thrust downward).
33. A rocket is freed once it is more than `0x500` pixels, 1,280, from the aircraft's drawn position (`0x1680` in the eighth-scale view). Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x010B04`–`0x010B40`.
34. The aim (`rocket_homing`, `0x01099A`): when the fall ends and the bearing points down, the ground the guns' formula gives for the bearing and `0x14` either side; the first gun of a ship afloat between them, else the first standing pillbox; aimed at what it found, the bearing becomes the angle of its distance and the rocket's height, turned down, and the speeds the airspeed over 100 along it. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The rocket's aim (read, and the oracle)"; `src/objects.c`, `homing`.
35. No script's rocket found anything to aim at: the pillboxes of `rockets_c` fell to rockets fired straight at them; the aim is held by the oracle. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The rocket's aim"; "Appendix: the regions no run executed" (`0x010A16`–`0x010A67`).
36. A rocket's frames: `ro01` to `ro0a` while it falls (`0x74` on), `rc01` to `rc0a` once it fires (`0x4C` on), by the bearing; ten more each facing west. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The object records in the pass"; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "The slots of the islands and the weapons"; `torpedo_names` read (counts table); the figure `weapons-torpedo`.
37. The torpedo falls like a bomb, drawn `tor2` or `tor7` by the side it faced at the drop (`0x88`, `0x89`). Source: `src/objects.c`, `object_draw`; `torpedo_names` (counts table).
38. Meeting the sea (low bits 0) no faster than 5 pixels a tick, the torpedo runs in it: `0x45000` a tick (about 4.3 pixels) the way it flew, for 200 ticks (`0xC8` in `+0x12`), with a splash at its x every tick, not drawn (frame `0x0A`), and it hits the first map record under it that is not sea. Faster, it hits the water and goes out there. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight" (observed: `torpedo_a` from 40 pixels goes out at once, `torpedo_run` from 24 runs its 200 ticks); `re/Wings.lst` `0x010D10` (`cmpi.w #$fffb`), `0x010D18`.
39. The manual asks for the torpedo to be dropped low over the water, with the ship in sight, so that it does not run out before it reaches the ship (page 7). Source: `original/manual.txt` page 7. Cited, not quoted.
40. A running torpedo that goes out is never drawn again, so its record is never freed until the hold clears all the records. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The object records in the pass" (read); `src/mission.c`, `wof_player_restart_state` (`wof_objects_clear`, `0x013756`). Sidebar at most.
41. Figure `weapons-flight.svg` (new, hand-drawn; its dots computed from `object_step`'s arithmetic, a dot a tick, not observed: the counts table gives the mirror's rules and starting values, so that a few dots can be recomputed): a bomb dropped level at 14 pixels a tick from a height of 150, a dot a tick to the ground, and the aircraft flying on; a rocket fired level (a fall of 8 ticks, then on its thrust, faster each tick) and one fired in a dive at 20 degrees (the thrust bringing it down); the ground at `0x0C` above the water line. Its numbers in labels: `0x0C`, 12, `0x6000`, a tenth, ten pixels a tick, 4, 8 or 12, `0x500`. The dots: the counts table.
42. Figure `weapons-torpedo.png` (`shapes` maker, `pick` with empty cells): `bom0` to `bomb`; `ro01` to `ro0a`, `tor2`, `tor7`; `rc01` to `rc0a`. Why: the bomb's twelve frames, the rocket's two sets and the torpedo's two are exactly the frames `object_draw` takes; chapter 12's sheet of the container (`shapes-torpedo.png`, in the specimen only) shows every shape, not these in order.

## 4. Where a weapon comes down

43. Each tick, before it tests the ground, `object_step` asks whether an airfield lies under the record (`0x011126`); over one, a rocket goes out and anything else bounces on the runway at height `0x1E`, the bounce chapter 5 told. Maps a to c have none. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight"; `book/docs/part-1/oracle.md` (the bounce; linked, not retold).
44. Elsewhere the ground is `0x0C`, 12 pixels above the water line, over the sea, the land and the targets alike, and a ship's deck plus `0x0B` over a ship (low bits 1). Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x010BF0`–`0x010C70`.
45. The listing tests the map record's low bits against the slots of the dug-out and the barracks, 3 and 4, a test of the wrong value that never holds, so their own heights (chapter 13's 9 and 13) play no part. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight" ("the listing's test of low bits 2 for the slots 3 and 4 never holds"); "Appendix: the regions no run executed" (`0x010C36`–`0x010C53` unreachable); `re/Wings.lst` `0x010C20`–`0x010C2C`; `book/docs/part-2/world.md` (the heights' table). The chapter says what the test does, not what was meant.
46. At the ground the record keeps the low bits (`+0x1F`), which choose the explosion over land or a deck and the splash over the sea; makes the impact's sound (chapter 18); notes the pass; calls `weapon_hit`; goes out, kind 8; and the running soldiers within `0x10`, 16 pixels, start dying. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: a weapon in flight"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x010C70`–`0x010CC6`.
47. Going out is eight frames of the explosion (`0x5A` on) or of the splash (`0x66` on), one a pass, and then the pass frees the record. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The object records in the pass"; `src/objects.c`, `object_draw`.

## 5. The targets

48. An island carries three kinds of target in its map records: slot 3, `dugo`, a dug-out that holds soldiers and fires at the aircraft; slot 4, `huta`, a barracks that holds soldiers and burns when hit, its records becoming slot 5, `hutb`; slots `0x0F` to `0x1E`, `pila` to `pilp`, a pillbox that fires and that only a rocket destroys. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Three kinds of target"; `src/targets.c`, the opening comment; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "The slots of the islands and the weapons".
49. The manual's names: barracks, machine-gun dug-outs and large antiaircraft guns, the last the target it gives the rockets for (pages 7 and 10); "pillbox" is the notes' name. Source: `original/manual.txt` pages 7, 10; `re/notes/porting-m5.md`. Cited, not quoted.
50. A target is four map records wide, and the third carries the draw flag (chapter 13); `target_records` (`0x014AE4`) finds the four from any x among them by looking for the flag at x − 8, x, x + 8 and x + 16. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Three kinds of target"; `src/targets.c` (`0x014AE4`).
51. Each kind has a table the loader built (chapter 13): `target_records_3`, `target_records_4` of `0x10` bytes and `target_records_f` of `0x0E`; a dug-out's or barracks' entry holds its place in the record list, its world x, the soldiers inside, its island and its counters. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "What the loader builds per mission" (the second walk: a state byte of 5 at `+8`, the island at `+9`); `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Three kinds of target".
52. Figure `targets-world.png` (`shapes` maker, `pick` with empty cells): `dugo`, `huta`, `hutb`; `pila` to `pilp`. Why these: the three slots of the dug-out and the barracks before and after a hit, and the sixteen pictures of a pillbox the hits make (claims 68, 69).
53. Each island keeps two counts in `island_score` (`0x025450`), two words an island: the soldiers still alive, five for every dug-out and barracks, and the pillboxes still standing. The loader adds 5 for each dug-out and barracks (`addq.w #$5,(a5)` at `0x01320A` and `0x01322E`) and 1 for each pillbox (`addq.w #$1,$2(a5)` at `0x013240`). Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Three kinds of target"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01312A`–`0x013240`; `src/globals.def` (`island_score`, eight words).
54. The barracks and the dug-outs are not counted as targets; an island is **neutralised** when both counts are 0, which the last soldier's death or the last pillbox's destruction finds. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Three kinds of target"; `src/targets.c`, `soldiers_draw` and `weapon_hit`.
55. The manual's rule: an island is neutralised when its barracks, soldiers and guns are all destroyed (page 10). The counts make it hold through the soldiers: a dug-out's or a barracks' soldiers inside it count until they are dead, a hit is the only way out for those of a dug-out, and a barracks gives a soldier to an empty dug-out only while it holds two or more, so its last soldier comes out only when it is hit. Source: `original/manual.txt` page 10 (cited, not quoted); `src/targets.c`, `0x015034` (`+8` at least 2), `0x014B40` (a hit lets them out), `soldiers_draw` (only the dead are taken off). *(The conclusion "every barracks must be hit": reasoning from the code, marked.)*
56. A dug-out's gun stops firing while it holds no soldier. Source: `src/targets.c`, `targets_3_draw` (`0x013D78`: an entry whose `+8` is clear draws no frame and fires not).
57. The island's flag beside the record of slot `0x113` waves while either count is above 0, and is a bare post once the island is neutralised. Source: `src/targets.c` (`0x013B52`); `re/notes/shapes.md` (`flg0` to `flg2`, `POST`); `book/docs/part-2/world.md` (the flag placed; linked).
58. Neutralised, the island gives its bonus from a table by map and island (`island_bonus`, `0x02347C`), counts one island fewer and puts its message on the ticker; the map's last island, with no enemy ship left, ends the mission (`mission_won`, `0x015694`), chapter 17's. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "Three kinds of target"; `src/targets.c`, `island_done` and `wof_mission_won`.
59. Map a's one island holds two dug-outs, at world x 1,736 and 3,224, and two barracks, at 2,392 and 2,712: twenty soldiers. Source: `tools/m5_autopilot.py`, the comment over `PLANS`; `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The scripts" (`bomb_a`, `island_a`: "until all twenty are dead").

## 6. What a hit does

60. `weapon_hit` (`0x0146DC`) is what an impact does to the map record under the object's x: it asks `map_slot_at` for the low bits and the slot, and leaves for the sea's and the ships' branch on low bits 0 or 1. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the hits"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x0146DC`–`0x014700`.
61. On land a rocket flashes the sky white, a count of 5 and the colour `0xFFF`, and red, `0xF00`, when it hits a target; a bomb never flashes. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The sky's flash (observed)", "The tick: the hits"; `book/docs/part-2/display.md` (the flash; linked).
62. The record of slot `0x113`, the island's flag, is left alone. Source: `re/notes/porting-m5.md`, "The tick: the hits"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01471A`.
63. A dug-out that holds soldiers takes the hit: 200 points, its refill timer set to 200, and its soldiers let out (`target_release`, `0x014B40`): those inside join those still to come out, and a timer starts at 60. A dug-out with none inside takes nothing. ## 9. The pools

112. ## Terms and glossary entries

New, in bold with an entry, "First met and defined in chapter 15." (no earlier chapter bolds or links them; chapters 5, 7, 8, 9, 13 and 14 say "dug-out", "barracks", "soldiers" and "pools" in plain text, which the build does not count):

- Object record: one of the fifteen records of 42 bytes in which a bomb, a rocket or the torpedo flies, with a sixteenth for the enemy's torpedo; its kind byte says whether it flies, goes out or is free, and its type which weapon it is. - Pillbox: the notes' name for an island's large gun, slots `0x0F` to `0x1E`, which fires and which only a rocket destroys. The detail: the same. Elsewhere: Wikipedia, "Pillbox (military)".
