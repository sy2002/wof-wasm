# Fact sheet: chapter 13, The world

Every claim the chapter makes, one line each, with its source, in the order of the chapter. A claim without a source goes to the list at the end and stays out of the draft. A general fact about the machine that the notes do not state is sourced in a reference work with its chapter and marked **(reference)**; what the game does with the machine always comes from the notes. Each mechanism ends with what the port made of it and, in one plain sentence, the instrument of Part I that holds it; no "How we know" box (`book/BOOK.md` 4, point 6; the controller's task).

Counts are of the branch's base, `346e056`, counted in the files named, by reading or with the command in the counts table at the end. Run for a fact: `tools/map_decode.py` over the fifteen maps (scratch scripts importing it, under a second each), `extract_tables.Image` for six words of the executable, `ppkc.parse` for shape sizes, and one run of `tests/test_map.py`'s `flight()` in the headless original (one core, 8 seconds) to count its passes. No test run, no browser, no rebuild of the native library.

Words used in the sheet as the chapter will use them: a **map offset** is a byte offset into the record list (record `i` at offset `2i`); world x is in pixels, record `i` covering world x `8i` to `8i + 7`; "row" is a screen row of the playfield counted downward from 0, as chapter 11 counts them.

## 1. A world in one file

3. A world is one file per mission, `maps/a.map` to `maps/o.map`: two longs, then a strip of two-byte records to the end of the file, one record for every eight pixels of world x; there is nothing else in the file. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file" and "The record" ("The whole map is that strip; there is nothing else in the file"); `SPEC.md` 3.5, "Maps".
4. `map_load` (`0x012ADC`) picks the file by the rank and the mission number, reads the two longs, allocates the record list and reads it, and calls `map_scan`. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file"; `re/notes/porting-m4.md`, "The mission setup" (the index: the missions of the ranks below plus the mission less one). Which map a rank and mission give is chapter 17's.
5. The first long is the file's own length in bytes, which the loader also takes as the length of the record list. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file"; `SPEC.md` 3.5; `tests/test_map.py`, `test_every_map_of_the_disk_parses_without_a_byte_left_over` (`chart.length == chart.file_length` for all fifteen).
6. The second long is a map offset that falls on the carrier's deck; four times it, less eight, is the world x where the player's aircraft starts (`player_start_x`, `0x025392`). Four times a map offset is the world x of its record, two bytes a record and eight pixels a record. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file" (`player_start_x` is the second long times four less eight); `src/mission.c`, `wof_map_load` (`start * 4u - 8u`); the test of claim 5 (`chart.player_x == chart.start * 4 - 8`); the place on the deck counted with `tools/map_decode.py` (counts table: in map a the long is 1,760, record 880, slot `0x20`, inside the carrier's records 826 to 918). See "Where a source was wrong or silent": the notes and `SPEC.md` call it the offset of the carrier's *first* record.
7. The loader derives the map's width, `map_extent` (`0x024630`), the world x past the last record, as the first long times four. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file"; `src/mission.c` (`length * 4u`). In the sheet; the chapter says the width is eight pixels a record.
8. Why the file needs no more: the record list is the world; every other table of the mission is built from it by the loader (section 6). Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "What the loader builds per mission" ("the only routine that turns map records into game objects").
9. The loader asks for and reads `length` bytes of records although only `length − 8` follow the header, so the last four records of every map never come from the file. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file" (bold paragraph); `SPEC.md` 3.5; `src/mission.c`, the comment on `wof_map_load`; `tools/map_decode.py`, the comment in `Map.__init__`.
10. Those four records are zero on the machine: the game's allocator, `0x020874`, through which every `mem_alloc` goes, sets bit 16 of the flags it hands exec's `AllocMem` (`bset.b #$10,d0` at `0x020884`), `MEMF_CLEAR`, which asks the system for cleared memory; `mem_alloc` and `mem_alloc_chip` already push `0x10001` and `0x10003`. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x020848`–`0x020888` (the two pushes and the `bset`); `SPEC.md` 7.2 (every allocation goes through `0x020874`, which sets `MEMF_CLEAR`, and the game relies on it).
11. `MEMF_CLEAR` is the flag of exec's `AllocMem` that asks for memory cleared to zeros, value `0x10000`, bit 16. **(reference)** *Amiga ROM Kernel Reference Manual: Libraries*, third edition, the chapter "Exec Memory Allocation" (the memory attributes); the include file `exec/memory.h` (`MEMF_CLEAR (1L<<16)`). `re/notes/map.md` names the bit so, and `book/docs/part-1/reading.md` already says the allocator "asks the system for cleared memory".
12. A zero record draws nothing and gives ground height zero, and the last record of the list is read by the draw loop but never drawn, so the four are harmless. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file"; claim 46 (the end test after the pointer has moved on); claim 60 (slot 0 answers zero).
13. Why the loader asks for eight bytes more is not established; nothing depends on the answer. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "Open", last point. The chapter says it once, plainly, as a fact about the subject.
14. For the port it is a rule: memory the game gets from the allocator must be zero, because the game relies on it here. The port keeps the record list in a fixed table of the core's state and clears it before the file is read into it, which is the same statement; its arena hands out zeroed memory too (chapter 22). Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The file" ("For the port this is a rule"); `SPEC.md` 7.2 (the arena hands out zeroed memory, and a test holds it; every table a mission allocates has a fixed place); `src/mission.c`, the file's opening comment and `wof_map_load` (`CLEAR(map_records); /* mem_alloc: MEMF_CLEAR */`); `src/mem.c`, `wof_alloc` and `wof_scratch_alloc` (`wof_mem_set(p, 0, aligned)`).
15. Chapter 4 already told how this finding corrected a note that blamed the machine. Source: `book/docs/part-1/reading.md` (the paragraph on the instrument's stand-ins). The chapter links, does not retell.
16. Listing `alloc_clear`: `sub_020874` from `0x020874` to `0x020888`, the size moved up by 12 and the `bset.b #$10,d0` before the call into `AllocMem`'s glue. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x020874`–`0x020888`. The 12 bytes are the allocator's own header and are not the chapter's point (named only if the listing's sentence needs it: `0x0208A2` stores the size at the start of the block).
17. The instruments: every map of the disk parses with nothing left over (the first long the file's length, four records past the file, the record count half the length, the width eight pixels a record, the start inside the map); and the map the first mission loads is, word for word, the file the decoder reads, with the loader's globals as the decoder computes them. Source: `tests/test_map.py`, `test_every_map_of_the_disk_parses_without_a_byte_left_over` and `test_the_map_the_first_mission_loads_is_the_one_the_loader_put_in_memory` (their asserts). Chapter 6's headless original runs the second.

## 2. The record, bit by bit

22. The record's sixteen bits: bit 15 the draw flag; bit 14 never set; bits 13 to 11 the height, 0 to 7; bits 10 to 2 the slot in `MasterList` or `AthList`, 0 to 511; bits 1 and 0, 0 for nothing, 2 for a record standing on the world, 1 for a record riding on a ship. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record", the table; `SPEC.md` 3.5; `tools/map_decode.py`, `fields`.
23. Listing `fields` (kind `py`, `tools/map_decode.py`): the five fields as the decoder reads them, a mask and a shift each. Source: `tools/map_decode.py`; `book/listings.toml` (the entry exists; first shown in a chapter here, the specimen page aside).
24. Bit 14 is set in none of the 34,473 records. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record"; counted (counts table: 0).
25. The draw flag: only a record that carries it puts its shape on the screen. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01385A` (`bclr.b #$d,d1` on the record shifted right by two, `beq` past the draw).
26. Why the flag: a shape wider than eight pixels covers several records, and every one of them carries its slot, while only one carries the flag, so the shape is drawn once. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record" (the paragraph after the table); `SPEC.md` 3.5.
27. Which column carries it: of the 2,847 drawn records standing on the world, 1,756 have two records of the same slot to their left, 664 three, 110 one and 317 none, the left neighbours counted back to the previous drawn record. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record"; recounted with `tools/map_decode.py` (counts table: identical with that rule). For a figures box; the prose says "most often the third".
28. A target's four records carry the flag on the third, which the routine at `0x014AE4` looks for. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record" (citing `re/notes/porting-m5.md`). The routine's purpose is chapter 15's; the chapter names no address for it.
29. Why every column keeps the slot: the ground-height routine reads the slot without looking at the flag, so a shape four records wide gives ground under all four columns while it is drawn once. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record" ("The other columns still carry the slot"); `re/Wings.lst` `0x015714`–`0x01571C` (`ground_height` takes `(a0)`, shifts and masks the slot, no test of bit 15).
30. Figure `maprecord-palm` (new maker `maprecord`): the four records 15 to 18 of map a, each `0x101A` except the third, `0x901A`: slot 6, `tre1` (a palm), height 2, low bits 2, the third with the draw flag; their sixteen bits in boxes grouped by field and named; the palm drawn over its 32 columns, 8 to a record, its hotspot on the third record's x. Source: `tools/map_decode.py --map a --list` (records 15 to 18, world x 120 to 151); `tre1` of `world.shp` 32 by 38, hotspot (16, 38) (counts table, `ppkc.parse`); `world_names` slot 6 `tre1` (read from the executable through `re/tables.toml`).
31. A drawn record's shape is drawn with its hotspot at the record's own world x: `draw_world` keeps the screen x plus 8 in D4 and draws at D4 − 8. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x0137F4`–`0x0137F8` (D4 starts at 8 − (x and 7) − 128) and `0x01389E` (`subq.w #$8,d0` before the hotspot is taken off); `tools/map_decode.py`, `draw_list` (returns `screen_x - 8`). In the figure; in the prose at most "at the record's own x".
32. The height: bits 13 to 11 put the shape that many steps of four rows below the horizon's row: at full scale `draw_world` draws a drawn record at the split row plus four times its height. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x013886`–`0x01388E` (`lsr.w #$7`, `and.w #$1c`, `add.w d5` with D5 the split row from `0x01383E`); `tools/map_decode.py`, `draw_list` (`split_row + HEIGHT_STEP * record['height']`); `HEIGHT_STEP = 4`. See "Where a source was wrong or silent": the notes and the decoder's comment say "above the horizon"; rows are counted downward, so the shape moves down.
33. Only the palms and the bump of land, slots 6 to 9, carry a height other than 0 in any map: 1,832, 1,488, 1,612 and 1,364 records; all heights 1 to 7 occur. So an island's palms stand on different rows. Source: counted with `tools/map_decode.py` (counts table); `re/notes/shapes.md`, "The slots of the islands and the weapons" (slots 6 to 9, `tre1` to `tre3`, `bumb`); visible in `map-a.png`.
34. In the eighth-scale view every drawn record stands on row 151, whatever its height. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x01387E` (`move.w #$97,d1` when `view_shift` is set); `tools/map_decode.py`, `draw_list` (`0x97 if view_shift`).
35. The slot names a shape in `MasterList` at full scale or `AthList` at the eighth scale, chapter 12's tables of 273 slots: the world's 184, then the four ships' blocks. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "MasterList and AthList"; `book/docs/part-2/shapes.md`. Linked, not retold.
36. A slot with no shape is skipped: its pointer is null. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "MasterList and AthList" ("skips a record whose slot is null", `0x01386E`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x01386E`–`0x013870`.
37. The tables are 278 entries long and only 273 are filled, so slots 273 to 277 are always null; the maps use three of them, `0x113` to `0x115`, as markers that draw nothing themselves: `0x113` beside each island's flag, `0x114` and `0x115` in pairs for an airfield. Source: `re/notes/shapes.md`, "MasterList and AthList" (`0x458` bytes, 278 longs, 273 used); `src/mission.c`, `wof_build_master_lists` (writes entries 0 to 272: 184 + 24 + 27 + 13 + 25); claim 10 (the allocation is cleared); `re/notes/map.md`, "The file" (slot `0x113` in every map with islands) and "Open" (`0x114`, `0x115`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x013B46` (`cmp.w #$113,d3` in `record_extras`) and `0x012CDE`, `0x012D18` (`airfields_scan`). The record counts per map in the counts table.
38. The low bits: 2 marks a record standing on the world, 1 a record riding on a ship, 0 nothing; 2,677 records of the fifteen maps carry 1, and every one of them belongs to a ship. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record" (observed); counted (counts table: low bits 0 in 14,235 records, 1 in 2,677, 2 in 17,561).
39. Why: a ship moves (it sinks), and its records must move with it while the record list itself does not. A drawn record with low bits 1 is moved down by the ship it stands on: `ride_on_ship` (`0x014EAC`) turns its x back into a world x, finds the ship with `ship_at_offset` (`0x014A4E`), which tests the map offset against each of the five ship records' spans (`+0` and `+2`), and adds that ship's `+0x1A`, the rows its records ride down by. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record", last paragraph; `re/notes/enemy.md`, "The ships", the record (`+0x1A` "the rows its records ride down by: the drawing's copy of the sinking"); `src/world.c`, `ride_on_ship`, `wof_ship_at_offset`.
40. Figure `map-a` (existing, first shown in a chapter): map a as the strip of its drawn records in bands of 960 pixels of world x, the island's palms, barracks and dug-outs on the left, the carrier on the right; the island's ground between its beaches is not a record and is not drawn. Source: `book/figures.toml`, `map-a` (`shows`, `how`). Why map a and not a later map: the first mission's map, the one chapter 1's picture comes from, and every one of its drawn records is a shape of `world.shp`; the maker draws no ship's block (slots from 184 on come from the ship containers, which the maker does not compose), and a later map is two to four times as wide; the map viewer shows all fifteen.

## 3. World coordinates

41. World x is in pixels along the strip from its west end, one record every eight; world y is a height in pixels, upward, zero at the water line. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "World coordinates".
42. `frame_update` computes the view once per pass from the drawing's copy of the player's position, which `snapshot_for_draw` takes between `Forbid` and `Permit`: `view_shift` 3 at the eighth scale, else 0; `view_x` the player's x less 160 shifted left by `view_shift`; `view_y` 151 plus whatever the player's y exceeds 131 by, or 1,208 at the eighth scale. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "World coordinates" (the text block); `re/notes/objects.md`, "The order of a pass" (`snapshot_for_draw`); `src/world.c`, `wof_frame_update`.
43. Why once per pass from a copy: every drawing routine of the pass then works from one position, whatever the tick does meanwhile. Source: `re/notes/objects.md`, "The order of a pass" (the copy is taken between `Forbid` and `Permit` and is what `draw_world` and `frame_update` build the view from). The chapter says what the copy gives; the tick's running beside the pass is chapter 7's.
44. `draw_world_shape` (`0x015174`) turns a world position into the screen's: screen x is world x less `view_x`, screen y is `view_y` plus 11 less world y, both shifted right by three at the eighth scale; a shape is drawn only while its screen x lies from −128 to 448. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "World coordinates"; `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines"; `src/world.c`, `wof_draw_world_shape`. The window is the routine's rule; the chapter claims no reason for it.
45. So at full scale one world pixel is one screen pixel and the aircraft is always at screen x 160; at the eighth scale `view_x` lies 1,280 behind the player and the aircraft is at 160 again. The water line is 11 rows below `view_y`; at full scale and low, row 162, at the eighth scale row 152. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "World coordinates"; `book/docs/part-2/display.md`, "Two scales" (water line at row 152 at the eighth scale). The horizon's ladder is chapter 11's: linked, not retold.
46. `draw_world`'s own loop over the map does the same arithmetic in its own words: it starts with the record `(x − 288) / 8`, x being the drawing's player x (at the eighth scale x rounded down to a multiple of 8 and the margin 2,304), puts it at screen x −128 less x's remainder modulo 8, steps the screen x by `view_step`, 8 at full scale and 1 at the eighth scale, and stops when the screen x passes 456, `0x1D0` less the 8 of claim 31; a record is read and drawn only while the pointer, moved on past it, has not passed the list's last record. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "World coordinates", last paragraph; `re/Wings.lst` `0x0137BC`–`0x0137F8`, `0x013842`–`0x01384A`, `0x0138B0`–`0x0138B8`; `tools/map_decode.py`, `MARGIN_FULL`, `MARGIN_EIGHTH`, `SCREEN_END`. So the loop covers the same window as `draw_world_shape`, from −128 to about 448 (counts table: the last record at screen x 448 or 449 at full scale, 455 at the eighth scale).
47. A record wholly before the map's start is stepped over without being read. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x013824`–`0x01383A`; `src/world.c`, `draw_world` (the comment). Sidebar or not at all.
48. Figure `world-view.svg` (new, hand-drawn): the playfield's 320 by 162 rows on a strip of records, the aircraft at screen x 160, the drawn range from −128 to 448 around the screen, `view_x` at the screen's left edge, the horizon's row `view_y` (151 when low) and the water line 11 rows below it with world y upward from it. Its numbers: 320, 162, 160, −128, 448, 288, 151, 11, all claims 42 to 46 and chapter 11's playfield.

## 4. A picture painted anew

49. There is no scrolling by copying: the playfield is redrawn from the map on every pass. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "Other drawing" (the last sentence).
50. A pass first fills the sky, colour 1, from the playfield's top down to row `view_y` + 1, at most 161, then draws the strip, then the layers. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines" (`draw_world`: sky, `rect_fill` colour 1 down to `view_y`); `re/Wings.lst` `0x013788`–`0x0137A4` (`view_y` + 1 clamped to `0xA1`, colour `moveq #1,d4`).
51. For each record of the strip: if it carries the draw flag, its slot's shape from `MasterList` or `AthList`, a null slot skipped, at its row (claim 32 or 34), moved with its ship if it rides on one (claim 39); then, drawn or not, the record's extras; then the next record. Source: `re/notes/drawing.md`, "The scene routines"; `re/Wings.lst` `0x013842`–`0x0138B8`; `src/world.c`, `draw_world`.
52. Listing pair: `draw_world` from `0x013842` to `0x0138B8`, as `draw_world_loop` (kind `asm`, `lines` as needed, about 41), beside `draw_list` (kind `py`, `tools/map_decode.py`), the same loop written out in Python. Source: `re/Wings.lst`; `tools/map_decode.py`, the module docstring ("`draw_list` is `draw_world`'s own loop ... written out").
53. A record's extras, `record_extras` (`0x013B1C`): a drawn record of slot 5, a burnt barracks, gets its smoke, at both scales; at full scale only, the carrier's flag beside the record of slot `0x22` (the tower), the carrier's lift beside the record of slot `0x9F`, and an island's flag beside the record of slot `0x113`. Source: `src/world.c`, `record_extras` (its comment and code); `re/Wings.lst` `0x013B46`; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "The slots of the islands and the weapons" (`hutb`, the burnt barracks; `flg0` to `flg2`, `POST`); `world_names` slot `0x22` `towr`, `0x9F` `fgyx` (read). The flag's states and which island it consults are chapter 15's.
54. Why extras ride on records: a flag or a lift belongs to a place of the strip, and the loop walks the places in view, so their drawing costs no search. Source: claim 53 (what the routine does). The chapter states what the arrangement gives, no designer's intent. *(reasoning from claim 53; candidate for the unsourced list if the fact-check objects)*
55. After the strip, the layers in their fixed order: the aircraft waiting on the carrier's deck, one for each life left but the one flying, at most eight; the aircraft on the carrier's lift; the player's aircraft; the enemy aircraft and the wrecks of those shot down; the guns of the dug-outs and the pillboxes; the enemy ships' guns; the aircraft parked on the airfields; the aircraft parked on the ships' decks; the sea's waves; and last the islands. Source: `re/Wings.lst` `0x0138BA`–`0x0138E2` (the eleven calls in order); `src/world.c`, `draw_world` and the comments of `deck_aircraft`, `lift_aircraft`, `wof_draw_player`, `wof_draw_enemy_aircraft`, `ship_guns_draw`, `airfields_draw`, `ship_planes`, `ocean`, `islands`; `src/targets.c`, the comments of `0x013D78` and `0x013DE8`; `re/notes/objects.md`, "The order of a pass". Then the smoke, the soldiers and the other pools of `frame_update` (chapter 15's).
56. The sea: at full scale five shapes of the wave animation from `world_shapes`, 96 pixels apart, placed from the player's x modulo 64, so that the waves move as the world moves; at the eighth scale a band of colour 10 from row 151. Source: `src/world.c`, `ocean` (its comment; `0x3F - (x & 0x3F)`, `+ 0x60`, `rect_fill(0, 0x97, 0x13F, 0xA2, 0x0A)`); `world_names` `0x4E` to `0x52`, `wava` to `wave` (read).
57. The islands: for each island its two beach ends, the shapes of slots 1 and 2 (`bchl`, `bchr`), at the world x of the drawn slot-1 and slot-2 records, which `map_scan` kept in two lists of four words, and a fill of colour 17 (`0x11`) between them from the horizon's row down. Source: `re/notes/map.md`, "Open", second point; `re/notes/shapes.md`, "The slots of the islands and the weapons" (slots 1 and 2, an island's west and east beach); `src/world.c`, `islands` (its comment); `re/Wings.lst` `0x014124` (`moveq #$11,d4`, per chapter 12's sheet claim 23).

## 6. What the loader builds

## Terms and glossary entries

Introduced in chapter 13, in bold with an entry; no earlier chapter links or bolds any of them (chapter 2 says "the ground's height" and chapter 3 "the map record" in plain text, which the build does not count), so each is "First met and defined in chapter 13":

- Map record: one word of a map file, two bytes for eight pixels of world x: the draw flag, the height, the slot and the low bits. The detail: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record"; `SPEC.md` 3.5.
- Draw flag: bit 15 of a map record; only a record that carries it draws its shape, one of the several records a wide shape covers. The detail: `re/notes/map.md`, "The record".
- World coordinates: positions in a mission's world, x in pixels from the map's west end, eight to a record, y in pixels upward from the water line. The detail: `re/notes/map.md`, "World coordinates".
- Ground height: the height of what stands at a map record, which the tick takes from the record's slot and height bits, or from a ship's record for a deck; the one thing a tick reads from the map. The detail: `re/notes/map.md`, "The ground height".
- Map decoder: `tools/map_decode.py`, the project's reader of the map files, written apart from the port, which predicts the map draws of a pass and is held to the original. The detail: `re/notes/map.md`, "The decoder and its controls".

Elsewhere lines: none planned; Wikipedia has no article that fits these game-specific terms closely enough ("Tile-based video game" was considered for Map record and left out: the records are not tiles drawn on a grid but columns of shapes drawn from one of them).
