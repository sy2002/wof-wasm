Words used in the sheet as the chapter will use them. A **sound slot** is one of the engine's eight records at `0x027368`, never "slot" alone (a slot is a shape's, chapters 12 and 15). A **channel record** is one of the four at `0x027E6E`. A **channel** is one of Paula's four. A **request** is an interrupt request, Paula's bit in `INTREQ`; an **interrupt** is a request that reached the processor. A **start** and a **restart** are the event log's words (`S`, the DMA switched on; `R`, a cycle's end that plays on). A **cycle** is one play of a channel's `2 x LEN` bytes. **The timer** is CIA-A's timer A; its tick is **the timer's tick**, never "tick" alone, which stays the logic tick; its latch is **the timer's latch**, never "latch" alone, which stays the fire button's (chapter 7). A song's fade is **the song's fade**, never "fade" alone, which stays the display's sixteen steps (chapters 7 and 11). A **period** is Paula's, in colour clocks. **The level-4 vector** is the long at `0x70`. A **round** of the fade's wait is one call of command 5 and the four VBlanks after it (`re/notes/headless.md`, "The fade's wait").

## 1. Paula, as this chapter needs it

4. Paula has four channels; each is given a sample's address (`AUDxLC`), its length in words (`AUDxLEN`, 0 being 65,536), a period (`AUDxPER`) and a volume 0 to 64 (`AUDxVOL`). Source: `re/notes/headless.md`, "The audio channels"; `book/docs/part-1/amiga.md` (Paula); `src/sound.c` (`AUD(c, r)` at `0x0A0 + 16c`).
5. A channel is switched on and off in `DMACON`, bit c for channel c; written with bit 15 set the named bits are set, without it they are cleared. Source: `src/audio.c`, `wof_paula_write` (`value & (1u << c)`, `value & 0x8000u`), `set_clear`; *Amiga Hardware Reference Manual*, chapter 7 **(reference)**.
6. Switched on, a channel takes `LC` and `LEN` and raises its request at once (Paula's first data fetch); it plays `2 x LEN` bytes, each for the period `AUDxPER` holds when the byte begins; at the cycle's end it takes `LC` and `LEN` again as they stand, raises the request again and plays on. So a program learns from the request when a sample has played, and can hand the channel the next part to play. Source: `re/notes/headless.md`, "The audio channels"; `SPEC.md` 6.5; `src/audio.c`, opening comment.
7. The request of channel c is bit 7 + c of `INTREQ`, `0x80` shifted by c; it reaches the processor only when `INTENA` has that bit and its master bit, bit 14 (`0x4000`); `INTENA` and `INTREQ` take the same set-or-clear rule of bit 15. `0x0780` names all four channels' bits. Source: `src/audio.c` (`request`: `0x80u << c`; `deliverable`: `P.intena & LEVEL4_ON` with `LEVEL4_ON 0x4000u`, `AUDIO_BITS 0x0780u`; `set_clear`); `src/sound.c`, `channel_play` (`1u << (c + 7)`); *Amiga Hardware Reference Manual*, chapter 7 **(reference)**.
8. The processor then runs the routine whose address lies at `0x70`, the level-4 vector; `sound_init` puts `audio_irq` there. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The channel layer" (`audio_irq`, "the level-4 autovector at `0x70`"); `src/sound.c`, `wof_sound_init` (the comment at `0x01E8FC`).
9. The four registers that can only be read, `DMACONR`, `ADKCONR`, `INTENAR` and `INTREQR`, ignore a write. Source: `re/notes/headless.md`, "The audio channels" ("The registers that can only be read"); `src/audio.c`, `wof_paula_write`.
10. Channels 0 and 3 go to the left, 1 and 2 to the right. Source: `book/docs/part-1/amiga.md` (Paula); `SPEC.md` 6.5. Linked, not retold at length.

## 2. Two layers

11. The **sound effects engine** has two layers, both in the executable: the sound slots beside the tick's routines (`0x011F4E`–`0x0123DA`) and the channel layer (`0x01E8B8`–`0x01ED78`). Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "Two layers"; `src/sound.c`, opening comment.
12. Why two layers (what the arithmetic does, no intent): the tick knows each tick what should sound; a channel can be given a new sample only at a VBlank, by the VBlank server, and learns that a sample has played only from its request, which only the interrupt handler sees; so the tick writes what it wants into the sound slots, and the channel layer starts and stops the channels when the hardware allows. *(Reading of the two code paths, marked.)* Source: claims 11, 22, 27, 33; `re/notes/sound.md`, "Two layers" and "The channel layer".
13. **Sound slot**, a term of this chapter: one of eight records of `0x18` bytes at `0x027368`, two to a channel, which the tick fills with what should play on that channel. The glossary's Slot is a shape's; the entry says the difference. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "Two layers" and "A slot"; `src/mission.def` (`WOF_TABLE(sound_slots, sound_slot, 8, 0x027368)`); `src/records.def` (`WOF_RECORD(sound_slot, 0x18)`).
14. A sound slot's fields: on (a word, set with `st.b`, which makes `0xFF00`, or copied from a flag word), the sample (a pointer), its length in bytes, the period, the volume 0 to 64, the repeat count (the cycles to play, -1 for ever); in the first sound slot of a pair also the sample the channel was last given, 0 for none, and its period and volume as one long. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "A slot" (the table); `src/records.def`.
15. **Channel record**, a term of this chapter: one of four records of `0x1E` bytes at `0x027E6E`, one a channel, which holds what the channel was asked to play: the sample (0 when free), the VBlank count when it last stopped, a flag while a start waits, the length in words, the period, the volume, the repeat count asked for and the count `audio_irq` takes down, and a volume easing no caller sets up. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "A channel record" (the table); `src/mission.def` (`WOF_TABLE(sound_channels, sound_channel, 4, 0x027E6E)`); `src/records.def`.
16. `sound_slots_init` (`0x011F76`), at the end of `sounds_load`, builds sound slots 0 to 6 from the sample pointers and lengths; sound slot 7 is filled by whoever switches it on. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The slots and what they play"; `src/sound.c`, `wof_sound_slots_init`.

## 3. From a sound slot to a channel

32. `sound_channels` (`0x012066`) runs once a tick, from `logic_tick`, and from `sound_slots_clear`. For each channel it takes the first sound slot of the pair that is on and has a sample; if that sample is the one the channel was given, it only adjusts the period and the volume, and only when they changed (`channel_adjust`, `0x01EB4C`); for another sample it calls `channel_play` (`0x01EA28`) three times with the same arguments; with no sound slot on, paused or with the music off, a channel that was given a sample is stopped three times (`channel_stop`, `0x01EAC0`). Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The channel layer"; `re/functions.csv` (callers `logic_tick`, `sound_slots_clear`); `src/sound.c`, `wof_sound_channels`; `re/Wings.lst` `0x0120A4`–`0x01211E`.
33. `channel_play`: no sample, nothing; a busy channel is stopped first; its request cleared and its interrupt enabled; the record takes the sample, the length in words (the byte length shifted right once), the period, the volume and the repeat count, and waits. Each stop stamps the record with the engine's VBlank count. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The channel layer"; `src/sound.c`, `channel_play`, `channel_stop`.
34. So the second and third calls stop the start the first set up and set it up again; whatever the first found, the triple call leaves the record stamped with the current VBlank count. *(Reading of `channel_play`: the second call finds the channel busy, since the first stored its sample.)* Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The channel layer" ("The second and third calls therefore stop the start the first one set up and set it up again; each stop stamps the record with the current VBlank count"); `src/sound.c`, `channel_play` (`if (CHAN(c).sample != 0) channel_stop(c)`).
35. `channel_stop`: its interrupt off, its DMA off, the record free, count -1, no easing, volume 0, the VBlank count stamped, and `AUDxPER` left at `0x7C`. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The channel layer"; `re/tables.toml`, `channel_stop_period` (`0x7C`, counts table). Sheet only, but for "stopped".
36. `soundfx_vblank` (`0x01EC64`), a VBlank server at priority 30, before `vblank_server` at -10: it counts the engine's VBlanks (`0x027E6A`); with the audio interrupts off, every record that waits and stopped at least two VBlanks ago gets `AUDxLC`, `AUDxLEN`, `AUDxPER` and `AUDxVOL` and its count, and its channel's bit is collected; one write of `DMACON` at the end switches them all on together; then the interrupts that were on, and all four. Source: `re/notes/sound.md`, "The channel layer"; `re/notes/headless.md`, "Scheduling" (the priorities); `src/sound.c`, `wof_soundfx_vblank`; `re/Wings.lst` `0x01EC9C`–`0x01ECEC` (`cmp.l #$2, d0`, `blt.w`, `or.w d1, sound_dmacon`), `0x01ED66` (the one `DMACON` write).
37. ## 8. A tick of a song

107. `SongInt` runs at every tick of the timer, after starting a waiting sound effect of the player's own (which the game never asks for), unless the music is paused; by `PlayState`: 0 idle, nothing; 1 a song asked for, each track from its sequence's first entry at volume 32, the channels' DMA off, then as 2; 2 playing, the four tracks' steps, and with no track that has started a note since the timer was opened the song is over; 3 a stop asked for; 4 fading, every speed + 1 ticks every track's volume one lower, with none left the song stops, otherwise the tracks play on. Source: `re/notes/music.md`, "The tick: SongInt" (the table); `re/songplay.lst` `0x02A6`–`0x02FC`; `src/music.c`, `wof_song_int`, `song_begin`, `song_step`, `song_fade`.
108. **Track**, a term of this chapter: one of a song's four lines of music, each on its own channel, a sequence of patterns played one after another; the player keeps a record of `0x4A` bytes for each. Source: `re/notes/music.md`, "The tick: SongInt" (`Track0Info` to `Track3Info`) and "The song format"; `src/records.def` (`WOF_RECORD(track, 0x4A)`); `src/music.c`, `song_step` (track t on channel t: `1u << t`).
109. A track's step (`track_step`, `0x04F4`): a note that sounds counts its length down; at its release tick the channel is switched off unless the note is tied or held; with the length run out, the pattern's next events are read (`track_read`, `0x05D2`) up to the next note, which `note_start` (`0x0704`) plays. Source: `re/notes/music.md`, "The tick: SongInt"; `src/music.c`, `track_step`, `note_release`, `track_read`.
110. Arpeggio and vibrato would move the period, but no voice of `wofsongs` has either on. Source: `re/notes/music.md`, "The tick: SongInt"; `tools/song_decode.py` (every voice: vibrato off, arpeggio off).
111. `note_start`: the note's length and release from the player's durations, the sample of the note's octave from the voice's sample, `AUDxLC` at the sample and `AUDxLEN` over its one-shot and repeat parts, the period from `note_period` (`0x07EA`), the track's volume, and DMA and the channel's interrupt on. Source: `re/notes/music.md`, "The tick: SongInt"; `re/songplay.lst` `0x0704`–`0x07E8`; `src/music.c`, `note_start`.
112. `CheckChannelInt` (`0x08AE`), from the player's level-4 handler, sets `AUDxLC` and `AUDxLEN` to the repeat part at the channel's first interrupt, which Paula raises at the start; the channel takes them at the end of its first cycle, so a sample plays its one-shot part and its repeat part once and then loops the repeat part; a sample without a repeat part is marked to go off at its next interrupt, the end of its one cycle. Source: `re/notes/music.md`, "The tick: SongInt" (**read**; **observed**: a start and then restarts at the repeat part's offset); `src/music.c`, `check_channel_int` (`tr->irq` 1, then -1, or 0 and off at the next); claim 6.
113. The period: `note_clocks` (DATA `0x1044`, 132 longs) gives per note the colour clocks of one cycle of the waveform, a semitone apart; the period is that divided by the voice's samples a cycle shifted by the octave. ## The terms

Defined in bold in this chapter, each with a glossary entry ("First met and defined in chapter 18", which the check computes, since no earlier chapter sets them in bold or links them): Sound effects engine (claim 11), Sound slot (13), Channel record (15), Interrupt request (claim 7's "request"), Level-4 vector (8), Event log (59), Music player (66), Song's fade (75), E clock (96), Timer's latch (97), Timer's tick (99), Track (108), Voice (117), Pattern (121).

## Where a source was wrong or silent

- The task's "the two writes to a register that can only be read": the player writes `INTREQR` three times, `0x04E2` (`audio_off`), `0x0A3C` (`_OpenTimerInt`) and `0x0A6C` (`_CloseTimerInt`), as `SPEC.md` 6.5 and `re/notes/music.md` ("Both routines ..., as does the stop path") say. `re/notes/headless.md`, "The registers that can only be read", names two ("songplay `0x0A3C` and `0x04E2`") and leaves out `0x0A6C`. A note silent; the controller may correct it at the merge.
- `re/notes/music.md`, "The game's calls": "`opt_music_off` has two writers". The loader is a third: the flag at `0x0254F7` is the last byte of a saved game's raw part (`tools/savegame.py`, `RAW_END = 0x0254F8`), which `save_game_read` writes back, as chapter 17 and `re/notes/keys.md` say for the flip's byte beside it. The note's conclusion holds: the music is unloaded whenever a load can set it (claim 89). A note silent; the controller may correct it.
- The task's "src/sound.c ... the never-called routines kept with a comment": the port carries none of the eight; `channel_play`'s comment names two of them (claim 51).
- The task's "1,129 starts and 10,219 restarts, 11,279 handler calls": 11,279 is `audio_irq`'s alone, over the effects; with the music the handler calls are 15,995 (claim 60).
- The task's "a long held note sounds at full volume to the end": the note says so of the drones of songs 2 and 4 (claim 77); the chapter keeps it to them.
- The task's figure (c) proposes song 4 or song 2: both open with three tied D#s of one voice, and song 2's fourth track with twenty whole notes of the rest voice; the figure shows song 0's first four bars instead, which hold a rest, a tie, a melody, a bass and two drum voices in 384 ticks (claim 131). Said in the report.
- The task's "(c) ... the rest voice shown as a gap": song 0's track 0 opens two of its first four bars with a half bar of the rest voice, which the figure shows.
- `re/notes/sound.md` names the files `Engine` and `Grind.1`; the disk holds `engine` and `grind.1` (`ls`), as the tests name them (`sounds/engine`). The chapter writes them as the disk does.
